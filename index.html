Masaryk Cultural Center5/24/26

Reimagining the statue of Tomáš G. Masaryk on Chicago's Midway Plaisance according to the Czechoslovakian leader’s vision of democracy and the city of Chicago’s Czech diasporic history. Specific project goals were to maintain large-scale site geometry, create symbolic vegetation & terrain, and provoke curiosity & research.

For Nootan Bharani’s Architecture of Memory, spring 2026, with Horn Ray Koh and Keyang Xie

Dedicated in 1955, the existing monument likens Masaryk to a Blaník Knight of Czech folklore, from a legend about knights who rest within a mountain until their country needs to be rescued. Though fading in condition, the monument has mattered to Chicago's Czech diaspora and to the University of Chicago, where Masaryk lectured.

I inverted the elevated plaza upon which the monument stands, bringing the statue closer to face-level. Drawing on contemporary Czech public sculpture, my hope was to encourage interactions with passersby on more equal ground.

The tiered green mounds nod to the Czech landscape and are designed to hold the ACASA archive underneath. ACASA stands for the 'Archive of Czechs and Slovaks Abroad' and is currently stored at the University of Chicago. It is the largest archive of its kind in the world, yet lacks a permanent space.

ISACream Stand4/17/26

An idea for a building dedicated to ice cream attached to the Institute for the Study of Ancient Cultures (ISAC).

For Nootan Bharani’s Architecture of Memory, spring 2026

Those enjoying an ice cream can head upstairs to the hemisphere to sit and watch students crossing the paths to get to class. Does the hemisphere look more like a cone or a scoop? Additional seating frames the exterior and conforms to the paths, taking the form of stone terraces evocative of zigurrats and pyramids inside the institute.

Since my first year of college, I had wished for a place that offered ice cream around campus. When I was assigned to design a space embodying the feeling of "joy," I couldn't resist the draw of an ice cream stand.

Pittsburgh World Consulate12/2025

A center for food and languages in Pittsburgh. The site is a parking lot north of the Allegheny, a short walk from the famous Randyland and the Mattress Factory.

For Luke Joyner and Casey Breen’s Revision, Expression & Portfolio Design, fall 2025

Not all embassies and consulates have to be government buildings. I imagined a kind of cultural embassy, a place where people from all over the world can come learn, teach, cook, and eat.

Not only the learning, but the teaching of languages should be accessible. Inspired by Pittsburgh's mission-driven organizations like Conflict Kitchen and City of Asylum, I designed a place that makes it easier to learn and teach languages across a myriad of formats spanning conference rooms, lecture halls, classrooms, outdoor amphitheaters, and community kitchens.
