Privacy policy

Noah Darwin Lee back

This is my personal website. I built it, I run it, and no one else has access to what it collects. This policy sets out everything the site collects, why it does so, and how you can have it removed.

Effective 24 November 2026. See also the terms of use.

1. In brief

If you only read the site, I learn nothing about you. Your visit adds one to a count of the day’s visits, and that count is only a number: it holds no address, no name, and no page. Opening citylayoutguessr adds one anonymous line a day recording that a browser opened the game. If you sign in to the game, I keep your name, email address, and profile picture so that your name and score can appear on the leaderboard; no one but me can see your email address. If you write in one of the site’s message boxes, I receive what you wrote, from which page, and when, and nothing more. If you make a donation, Ko-fi handles the payment, never this site. If you would like anything removed, write to me and I will delete it. CHRONIA, my atlas at chroniaatlas.com, has its own privacy policy.

2. Who is responsible

“I” and “me” in this policy mean Noah Darwin Lee. I run noahdarwinlee.com and every page under it (the “site”), and I decide what the site collects and why. In data protection terms, that makes me the controller of everything described here. There is no company behind the site, only me. If you have a question about anything in this policy, please write to me at noahlee519@gmail.com.

3. Reading the site

The pages are plain files, with no cookies, no analytics, and no advertisements. The only thing I measure is how many visits the site receives. When you open a page and your browser has not opened one here in the last thirty minutes, it asks the site’s database to add one to the day’s total. The database keeps the date and that number, and nothing else: not your address, not the page, and not where you came from. So that it can tell a new visit from a click to the next page, your browser remembers when it last opened a page here (section 9).

A few services necessarily see the ordinary request your browser makes to show you a page, and that request includes your IP address and the kind of browser you use. GitHub Pages hosts the site and its typeface. Google Fonts serves the type in the blog’s mail window and in GREATERCIRCLE. One tool loads drawing libraries from cdnjs, which Cloudflare runs. When you zoom in on the satellite view in GREATERCIRCLE, the sharper imagery comes from NASA’s Global Imagery Browse Services. Drill looks up each figure’s picture on Wikipedia and loads it from Wikimedia. I do not ask any of these services to identify you, and none of them reports anything back to me. Each handles those requests under its own privacy policy.

4. Playing citylayoutguessr

The visit record. The first time your browser opens the game on a given day, the game records one line noting that a visit happened: the kind of event, the date, the page address, and the page you came from, if your browser provides it. It is written once per browser per day and contains no IP address and no name. If you are signed in, it also holds your account identifier; if you are not, nothing in it points to you.

Everything else. Your round in progress, your settings, your colours, and your result on the day’s puzzle all stay in your own browser (section 9), and none of it is sent anywhere.

5. Signing in

Signing in to the game is optional; it puts your name beside your score on the leaderboard. You sign in with your Google account, through Supabase, which runs the sign-in.

What I receive. When you sign in, Google gives the site your name, email address, profile picture, and Google account identifier, and Supabase stores them as your account. The game copies your name and picture into a profile, and you may replace the name with any display name you like.

What others can see. Your display name, your profile picture, your daily scores, and your place on the two leaderboards. Anyone who opens the game can see these.

What they cannot. Your email address. It is stored with your account, and only I can see it, through the database’s own dashboard.

6. Scores

When you finish the daily challenge while signed in, the game records one line: your account identifier, the date, how many of the ten you answered correctly, how long you took, and when the score was posted. There is one line per person per day. These lines make up the leaderboards, so they are public in the way section 5 describes. Custom games are never posted.

7. Messages

Several pages, including the front page, the projects page, the blog, and the art page, have a box in which you can tell me what you think. When you send something, I store what you wrote, which page you sent it from, and when it arrived, in a Supabase database. That is all: no name, no email address, and no account, unless you type one into the message yourself. Supabase sees the ordinary request your browser makes, as the services in section 3 do, but I keep nothing from it with the message. Only I can read what you send; the site can add a message but can never read one back.

8. Donations

The donate link in citylayoutguessr leads to my page on Ko-fi. If you give something, Ko-fi and the payment service you choose there, Stripe or PayPal, take your payment and your details under their own privacy policies, and this site never sees your card or account. Ko-fi shows me the name you give, your email address, and any message you leave, and I use them only to keep track of donations and to thank you.

9. What stays in your browser

Some things are saved in your own browser’s storage so that the site remembers them next time. Everywhere: your choice of colour theme, and when you last opened a page, for the visit count. In the game: a round in progress, your custom setup, your colours, the leaderboard tab you last opened, your result on the day’s puzzle and whether it has been posted, the date of your last visit, and, if you have signed in, the token that keeps you signed in. In the flash card tool: your lists, drafts, and colours. On the blog: which letters you have read or starred, and how you sized the panes. In Drill: your progress and settings in each deck, and the addresses of the figures’ pictures, for a week.

All of this stays on your device. None of it is sent to me or to anyone else, and clearing your browser’s data for noahdarwinlee.com removes it. The sign-in token is the one exception: your browser shows it to Supabase to prove that it is you, and that is its only purpose.

10. The legal basis

Where the law asks for a legal basis, these are mine. I keep your account, profile, and scores because you chose to sign in and post them; that choice is your consent, and you may withdraw it at any time by asking me to delete your account. I keep a message because you chose to send it, and a donor’s details because they are needed to record the donation. The game’s daily visit record rests on my legitimate interest in knowing, roughly, whether anyone plays the game, and it serves that interest without identifying anyone who is not signed in. The site’s visit count rests on my interest in knowing, roughly, how many people read the site.

11. Who else is involved

Besides the services in section 3 and Ko-fi in section 8, three more take part. Supabase runs the database and sign-in for the game and keeps the messages from the site’s message boxes. The game’s copy of Supabase’s script is delivered by jsDelivr. Google handles the sign-in itself. These services may store and process information on servers outside your country, including in the United States.

I do not share, rent, or sell anything the site collects. There are no advertising networks, tracking pixels, third-party analytics, or mailing lists. The only circumstance in which I would hand anything over is if the law required me to.

12. How long I keep things

I keep accounts, profiles, and scores for as long as the game exists, since a leaderboard with a history is much of the point, or until you ask me to delete them. I keep the game’s visit records as a running account of how it is played, and the site’s daily visit totals as a running account of how much it is read. When I delete an account, everything tied to it is deleted with it. I keep messages until I have read them and no longer need them, and never for more than a year.

13. Your rights

You may sign out at any time and change your display name from the game’s menu. Beyond that, you may ask me to:

Show you. Send you a copy of everything the site holds about you.
Correct it. Fix anything that is wrong.
Delete it. Remove your account, your profile, and every score attached to it.

Please write to me at noahlee519@gmail.com from the address you signed in with, so that I know the request is really yours. I will deal with it as soon as I see it, and always within thirty days. These rights are yours wherever you live. If you are somewhere whose law grants them formally, such as the United Kingdom, the European Union, or California, this is how to exercise them, and you may also complain to your local data protection authority.

14. Security

The site is served over HTTPS. The game’s database lets a browser add a visit record but never read one back, lets each person post only their own score, and lets only you change your own profile. No system is perfectly secure, however. If anything ever went wrong that affected your information, I would tell you.

15. Children

The site is not intended for children under 13, and I do not knowingly keep information about anyone under 13. If you believe a child has signed in, please write to me and I will delete the account.

16. Changes to this policy

If what the site collects changes, this policy will change with it and the date at the top will move. I will never publish a version of this policy that quietly allows me to do more than the one you are reading.
