GREATERCIRCLE

Calculates the shortest path between two points on Earth, avoiding selected regions

The idea came from the seat-back map. It is often out of date, and now and then alarming: a line drawn straight across a war zone the plane will go nowhere near, or a path that was closed months ago. This is a way to see the route an airline actually has to think about: the shortest way between two places, and the shortest way once the closed skies are taken out.

Shortest paths around the chosen countries are exact for the drawn borders: a route of great-circle arcs that bends only at their convex corners, found by A* over the tangent visibility graph. Distances are on a sphere of radius 6,371.0088 km.

Borders: Natural Earth, countries at 1:50m and their subdivisions at 1:10m (public domain). Places: GeoNames (CC BY 4.0), OurAirports (public domain), and world-countries (ODbL); the place list here is derived from them under the same terms. What they lack is looked up as you type at Photon by komoot, whose answers are © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).
