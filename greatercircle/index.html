GREATERCIRCLE

An interactive globe built for anyone curious about how we really fly. While standard flight maps draw straight lines from point A to point B, this tool calculates the true great-circle distance between any two points, factoring in political geography and user preference. Simply place your points, pick the regions or airspace to avoid, and watch the program plot out the optimal route.

Shortest paths around the chosen countries are exact for the drawn borders: a route of great-circle arcs that bends only at their convex corners, found by A* over the tangent visibility graph. The buffer keeps the route at least that far from everything avoided, 50 miles unless you choose otherwise, and never more than about 3% further. The flight plan goes from one radio beacon (a VOR or an NDB) to the next, as a flight plan does, in legs of at most 300 km, longer only across sea or desert with no beacon near the way, and is the shortest such chain round what you avoid. Flight times assume 850 km/h and half an hour to climb and descend, and with winds on, the month's typical winds at cruising height (an average, not a forecast). Distances are on a sphere of radius 6,371.0088 km.

          Units

          Direct is the great circle: the shortest way between two places on a sphere, and the line a plane would fly if nothing were in the way.

          Avoiding is the shortest way that stays out of the countries and regions you chose, buffer included. It is still made of great-circle arcs, and it only bends where it has to, at the corners of what it goes round.

          Flight plan is closer to how airliners really fly. They don't follow one smooth arc. They fly from waypoint to waypoint, and the flight plan, filed before departure, is the list of them. The oldest waypoints are radio beacons on the ground (VORs and NDBs) that a plane's instruments can home in on. This route hops from beacon to beacon in legs of at most 300 km, longer only over open sea or desert, and still keeps clear of what you avoid, so it comes out a little longer than the way round.

          Real flight plans also use waypoints that are just named coordinates, airways, and the day's winds. This one leaves those out.
