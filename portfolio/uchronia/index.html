Technical details

The globe and the flat map are drawn on a canvas from Natural Earth’s states and provinces, with geoBoundaries’ counties and districts for 40 large countries, so a border can follow a county line. Each country’s name is fitted to its shape and, in the Antique style, bent along it; rivers, lakes, 3,000 cities and 40,000 towns are placed so that nothing collides.

The historian is Claude, behind a small server function that holds the key. It reads a plain list of how your map differs from ours, and redraws by sending back edits in a fixed shape, regions changing hands, new countries, subjects, and capitals, which the page applies as one step that a single undo takes back. A shared map travels inside its own link, so nothing is stored on a server.

  • 3,699 regions
  • 18,000 counties
  • 40,000 towns
  • Canvas and WebGL
  • Claude

Inspiration

I have been fond of atlases, world history, alternate history, and cartography for as long as I can remember. I built the tool I had always wanted for making my own maps and fictional worlds. I hope UCHRONIA encourages people to learn more about the world, both the real one and the imagined one.

Who it’s for

Writers and worldbuilders

  • Drawing the map of an alternate-history novel or screenplay, then asking how it came to be.
  • Testing a premise, such as a surviving Austria-Hungary, against its consequences on other continents.
  • Keeping a world’s map and its history side by side, and sharing both as one link.

Teaching and learning

  • Showing how one war or treaty redrew borders far beyond where it was fought.
  • Comparing the world as it is with the world a different decision would have made.
  • Learning where the world’s states, provinces, and capitals actually are, by moving them.

History enthusiasts

  • Settling “what if” debates with a map instead of an argument.
  • Drawing the borders of a past year, colonies and subjects included, and comparing them with today’s.
  • Asking follow-up questions about a world, and getting the map changed when the history demands it.

Games and play

  • Mapping the world of a tabletop campaign or a grand-strategy game run.
  • Sketching the end state of a game of Diplomacy or Risk on the real world.
  • Making a printable-looking antique map of a world that never was.
