# CHRONIA's ledger

One entry per change to the maps, newest first, as [MAPMAKING.md](MAPMAKING.md#the-ledger) describes. A claim in progress is an entry with "Claimed by" and the date, replaced by the finished entry when the work is done.

Each entry:

```
## <years> | <region> | <polity or subject>

- What changed: …
- Tables: …
- Sources: (1) … (2) …
- Confidence: high, medium, or low, because …
- Alternatives: …
- Left out: …
```

## 476–1444 | Vorarlberg, the Mühlviertel, and Jersey | Bregenz and its neighbours out of the Avars' and Hungary's land, and St Helier as Jersey's capital

- What changed: Vorarlberg's four districts lay west of the polygon that gives Austria's Bavarian land, so they fell to the row for Austria east of the Enns and were drawn as the Avars' from 568 and Hungary's from 907 to 1444, which Noah noticed at Bregenz. Bregenz and Dornbirn now follow Alemannia (the Alemanni, the Franks from 746, East Francia, and Swabia from 915, the Empire's own from 1268), and Feldkirch and Bludenz follow Raetia Curiensis as the Grisons do, passing to the Habsburgs (Further Austria) in 1379 and 1420. The Mühlviertel north of the Danube had the same fault: Urfahr now follows Bavaria, and the Riedmark (Perg and Freistadt), east of the Enns's mouth, is the Avars' and the Magyars' only until Lechfeld (10 August 955) and Bavaria's after. Jersey's capital, shown from 1400 to 1460 and after as "Government House" (the Lieutenant Governor's residence, which Wikipedia's infobox lists beside St Helier), is now St Helier.
- Tables: `tools/medieval.mjs` (the rows for Vorarlberg and the Mühlviertel after `AUT#carantania`); `tools/medieval-high.mjs` (Feldkirch and Bludenz to `HABS`); `tools/build-eras.mjs` (`CAP_DROP`: Jersey's Government House and Saint Saviour).
- Sources: (1) Austria-Forum's articles on Feldkirch (sold by Rudolf V of Montfort to Leopold III in 1375, his bailiffs in the town from 1379) and Bludenz (to Austria in 1420); Wikipedia's County of Werdenberg (Bludenz sold in 1394); the Vorarlberg history of the Vorarlberg schools' site (Alemannic settlement from about 500); the Historisches Lexikon der Schweiz on Raetia. (2) Wikipedia's Bregenz, Vorarlberg, Mühlviertel, and Riedmark; Wikidata's capital of Jersey (Q785, Saint Helier). Places from UCHRONIA's units.
- Confidence: high that Vorarlberg was never Avar or Hungarian; medium for the line between Alemannia and Raetia (drawn on district lines, with Dornbirn Alemannic) and for the Riedmark's years.
- Alternatives: Bludenz Habsburg from the sale of 1394 (Wikipedia) rather than 1420; Feldkirch from the sale of 1375 or Rudolf V's death in 1390 rather than 1379; the Riedmark Bavarian from 955 or only from the march's growth in the 970s.
- Left out: Bregenz's half sold to the Habsburgs in 1451 and the rest in 1523, after the table's end; Cliopatria's Kingdom of Spain at Bregenz from 1529 to 1555, for Ferdinand I's Tyrol and Vorarlberg, which is left as the sources give it.

## 1101–1250 | Europe, the Maghreb, Anatolia, the Levant, and the Caucasus | the medieval table's second stage, its wars, and its towns

## 3400 BC–1 BC | the world | peoples, cultures, bands, cities, and the kinds of border

- What changed:
  - **Blank land.** Most of the world was blank before 1 BC in some years: 108 million km² of land in the map of 3000 BC's years (3400 to 2501 BC), and 44 to 56 million in the years of the maps of 323, 300, 100, and 1 BC, which leave out peoples the maps around them draw. Bands of hunter-gatherers on any BC snapshot now fill the blank land of every BC snapshot's years, nearest first; nineteen peoples are carried into the next snapshot's years where it leaves them out (`PEOPLE_CARRY`); and 60 archaeological cultures and peoples and two states that neither source draws are added on Natural Earth's first-level units (`EARLY`), listed in the README's [Before 1 BC](README.md#before-1-bc).
  - **Peoples where they weren't.** historical-basemaps' Illyrians of 1000 BC in central Italy, Tumulus and Urnfield cultures in Britain and Ireland, and Tai-Kadai peoples of 2000 BC in northern China give way to the Italic, British, and Chinese cultures (`over`). ## all years | world | stray border lines inside states, as in the Holy Roman Empire

- What changed: short border lines no longer float inside states, as they did inside the Holy Roman Empire from 1400 on and in many other places.
