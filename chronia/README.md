# CHRONIA

An atlas of the world's borders from 3400 BC to today. A year control at the foot of the page moves the map through time, on a globe or a flat map, in the Antique style of [UCHRONIA](../uchronia/). Clicking a state, a people, or a city opens a card with what the maps say about it and a short note on it at that date, written by a language model (DeepSeek, or Claude when DeepSeek is unavailable) and kept for everyone who opens the same card after, with a link to its Wikipedia article. Until the note's server function is deployed, the card shows the opening of the Wikipedia article instead (see [Deploying the notes](#deploying-the-notes)).

Until 1885 the borders change year by year, from the Cliopatria dataset, which begins in 3400 BC, with the land it leaves blank filled from dated snapshot maps. From AD 106 to 117 the Roman Empire is drawn with its provinces. From 1886 they are dated to the day, so every month can be shown. States show their capitals from 1400: from Wikidata until 1885, and from CShapes after. In every period the map shows the cities of the time, ranked by their size then and named as they were then, and, for some countries, their first-level divisions, such as the states and territories of the United States. The land, lakes, and rivers are today's, except for a list of large, well-dated changes, such as the Zuiderzee, the Aral Sea, the great reservoirs, and the Suez and Panama Canals.

CHRONIA is the real-history companion to UCHRONIA, which redraws history. The two share the same look, the same base geography, and the same rendering approach. CHRONIA has its own page, data, and code, and it reads some of UCHRONIA's files in place (see [What it borrows from UCHRONIA](#what-it-borrows-from-uchronia)).

- **Live:** https://noahdarwinlee.com/chronia/, linked from UCHRONIA's about note
- **Adding to the maps:** read [MAPMAKING.md](MAPMAKING.md), the rules for sources, historiography, and method, and record each change in [LEDGER.md](LEDGER.md).
- **First built:** 25 October 2026.

## Using it

- **The year:**
  - Drag the marker along the scale.
  - Type into the box: `1815`, `500 BC`, `-500`, `AD 800`, `1950`, `June 1991`, `25 June 1991`, `1991-06`, and `6/1991` all work.
  - ← and → go to the previous or next change of borders: the previous or next year in which a border changed until 1885, and the previous or next dated change from 1886, shown with its day ("30 June 1960"). With Shift, they move by one year before 1886, and by one month from 1886.
  - Two play buttons at the bottom left play through time. The first, or P, plays at the normal pace; the second, marked with a double triangle, or Shift P, plays at double speed. Each turns into a pause button while it plays, and only one plays at a time: pressing the other switches the pace without stopping.
  - The normal pace is change by change until 1200, from each year in which a border changed to the next, 0.8 seconds apart (about four and a half minutes from 3400 BC); then year by year to 1885, a quarter of a second a year, with a year in which the borders change held for 0.7 seconds; then month by month from January 1886 to December 2019, the end of the data. A month takes 60 ms, about a year and a half a second, and a month in which the borders change stays for 0.4 seconds, so each change can be seen. 1200 to 1885 takes about five minutes and 1886 to 2019 about three and a half, where the busy years take longest: 1960 about three and a half seconds, a quiet year under one. Double speed halves every one of these times.
- **The map:** drag to turn the globe or move the flat map. Scroll, pinch, the + and − buttons, or the + and − keys zoom. A double-click zooms in on the spot. M switches between the globe and the flat map.
- **A state:** hover to see its name and, for a dependency, its ruler. With first-level divisions shown, the name is the division's and the state's, "Bavaria, German Empire". The name has a small flag before it for a state, and a dotted outline for a division; a people has none. The icons are small line drawings in the tooltip's ink (`TIP_ICONS`, set by `setTip`). Click it to open the card. Esc closes the card.
- **A city:** hover over a city's mark or name to see its name and the state it is in at the date, "Lutetia, Roman Empire", and, if its name has changed, its name today. The name has a small drawing of buildings before it. Click the mark or the name to open the same card for the city.
- **Search:** the search button at the top right, or the / key, opens a box that finds what is on the map at the date shown: states and peoples, cities and towns under their names of the time (and their names today), seas and oceans, lakes of 150 km² or more, and marshes. Up to eight matches are listed, best first; ↑ and ↓ move through them, and Enter or a click flies the map there and, for a state or a city, opens its card. Esc closes the box. While time is playing, the button is greyed out and the box can't be opened, since the map changes under it; pausing brings it back.
- **Layers:** the layers button at the top right opens a list of what the map shows: the names of states and peoples, capitals, cities and places, the names of seas and oceans, rivers and lakes, terrain relief, lines of latitude and longitude, and first-level divisions. Each can be turned off and on, and the map redraws at once. The choices are kept in the browser, not in the address. Esc closes the list.
- **Key:** the key at the bottom left, above the date, explains the map's signs in the Antique style: a capital (ringed mark), a city (dot), a town (small dot), a border (solid line), an approximate border (dotted line), a band with no fixed border (a faint italic name), a dependency (in its ruler's colour, with the ruler named on hover and in the card), a member of a larger state, a state of the Holy Roman Empire or a province of the Roman Empire (in the larger state's colour, with a fine broken line between members), a marsh or fen before it was drained (reed marks on a wash of water colour), a people or culture (uncoloured, italic name), and a first-level division (fine broken line). It shows only the rows that apply to the map on screen and to the layers that are on (see [The key](#the-key)). The "Key" bar folds it away and opens it again; the choice is kept in the browser as `chronia-key`. Without a saved choice it starts open, and closed on phones and on screens under 600 px tall. On a phone it is hidden while the card is open.
- **Links:** the address keeps the time and the view (`#y=1815&v=10.00,48.00,4.00&flat=1`). From 1886 the time is a month, `#y=1991-06`, or a day, `#y=1991-06-25`, so a link opens the same map.

### The page

The top of the page is one row, `.ch-top`, laid out as a grid: the title on the left and the map controls on the right, with the space between them left to the map. Both are framed the same way, with `.ch-frame`: a rule, a fainter inner rule, the parchment, and a soft shadow, at one height (`--ch-bar`). The title stands alone in its frame, with no subtitle. The info button is the last part of the title's frame, and the zoom, flat map, and layers buttons are parts of one frame, divided by faint rules. The about note, the layers list, the key, and the card use the same frame. On phones the zoom buttons are hidden, and the title's frame stretches to the controls.

The year bar holds the two play buttons, the date, and the scale. On phones the date is centred between the two play buttons, with the scale below.

**The opening.** When the first map is ready, the page opens as UCHRONIA's Antique style does: a sheet of parchment with a paper roll at its edge slides off to the right over 1.3 seconds and uncovers the map, the top row drops in and the year bar rises from below, each with a small bounce, from 1.35 seconds, the header's buttons pop in one by one from 2.25 seconds, and the key fades in. `playIntro()` adds `ch-intro` to the page for three seconds and the `.ch-roll` sheet; the sheet moves by `translate`, which the browser animates off the main thread, so it stays smooth while the first map's names are laid out. With reduced motion, the page appears at once.

`layout()` writes the height of the year bar to `--ch-time-h`, so that the card and the about note on a phone sit directly on top of it.

### Layers

`layers` holds one flag for each layer (`states`, `capitals`, `cities`, `seas`, `water`, `relief`, `grid`, `subdivisions`). Each place that draws a layer checks its flag first:

- capitals: `placeOne`, after the names of states;
- cities: `placeOne`, from `drawPlaces`;
- the names of states and peoples: `drawPolityLabels`;
- the names of seas: `drawSeaLabels`;
- rivers and lakes: `drawLakes` and `drawRivers`;
- relief: the relief overlay in `draw`;
- latitude and longitude: the graticule in `drawSea`;
- first-level divisions: `drawDivisionLines` and `drawDivisionLabels`, and the line on the card from `syncCardDivision`.

Changing a flag clears the cached sea layer and relief, then redraws. The flags are saved in `localStorage` as `chronia-layers`.

### The key

The key is the `#key` section in `index.html`, one `<li data-key="…">` per row, each with a small SVG sample drawn to match the map: the capital's mark at 4.3, 3.2, and 1.35 px, the city's dot at 1.85 px and the town's at 1.4 px, each with its halo; a 1.15 px border between two colours with their bands at 0.6 opacity; the dotted border as 0.1 px dashes 3.05 px apart with the softer bands; the dependency's faint inner line; the broken line between members of a larger state; the marsh's reed marks; disputed land as stripes of two colours, from an SVG pattern turned 45°; and the division's broken line. The colours come from the same `--ch-` properties the canvas reads.

`KEY_ROWS` in `app.js` holds one test per row, given the current map, and `syncKey()` hides the rows whose test fails:

- capital: with the capitals layer on, from 1886, and from 1400 to 1885 when a state on the map has a capital;
- city: from a scale of 230 px for the globe's radius, where the page starts drawing marks, with the cities layer on;
- town: from city tier 2 (a scale of 480 px), with the cities layer on;
- border: when any shape on the map is drawn with a firm border;
- approximate border, dependency, disputed land, and people: when the current map has any;
- band: when the current map has a band of hunters, gatherers, or fishers (see step 3 of [Building](#building)).
- member of a larger state: when the current map has a member of the Holy Roman Empire or a Roman province;
- marsh: only when a marsh was drawn in the last frame;
- first-level division: only when division lines were drawn in the last frame.

`syncKey()` runs whenever the date or a layer changes, and in each frame just after the division lines are drawn, so the division row follows the zoom and the layer. To add a row, add an `<li>` and a test. The key's box is one of the page's panels in `fixedBoxes()`, so names on the map keep clear of it.

### What it borrows from UCHRONIA

CHRONIA loads these from `../uchronia/` rather than copying them, so they are not duplicated in the repository:

- `vendor/d3-array.min.js`, `vendor/d3-geo.min.js`, and `vendor/topojson-client.min.js`;
- `sat.js`, the WebGL layer, which CHRONIA uses only for terrain relief;
- `data/relief-4096.webp`, `data/sea.json` (deep water), `data/rivers.json`, and `data/lakes.json`.

Renaming or reshaping any of these in UCHRONIA breaks CHRONIA. The builders also read UCHRONIA's files: `data/land.json` and the divisions outside the United States are built from `uchronia/data/provinces.json`, `data/land-detail.json` from it with `uchronia/data/provinces-detail.json`'s arcs, and the cities from `uchronia/data/cities.json`, so a change to UCHRONIA's coastline, provinces, or cities reaches CHRONIA only after a rebuild.

`data/geography.json` refers to `data/land.json`, `data/land-detail.json`, UCHRONIA's `data/lakes.json`, and UCHRONIA's `data/rivers.json` by their contents: a polygon of the land by its first point and length, a lake by its name and area, and a river by its index, first point, and length. When any of the three is rebuilt, rebuild the geography too. Anything that no longer matches is left as it is today rather than changed wrongly.

## The data

### Source

**Snapshot maps:** [historical-basemaps](https://github.com/aourednik/historical-basemaps) by André Ourednik, one GeoJSON file per year. Its 39 maps from 3000 BC to 1880 are no longer shown as maps of their own: they fill the land that Cliopatria leaves blank (see [Until 1885](#until-1885)). They are

3000, 2000, 1500, 1000, 700, 500, 400, 323, 300, 200, 100, and 1 BC; AD 100 to 1100 by centuries; and 1200, 1279, 1300, 1400, 1492, 1500, 1530, 1600, 1650, 1700, 1715, 1783, 1800, 1815, 1878, and 1880.

Until 27 October 2026 the 23 maps to 1100 were shown as maps, and the yearly data began in 1200 (in 1400 before that). Its maps from 1900 on are not used.

In historical-basemaps, each shape has a name (`NAME`), the power it is subject to (`SUBJECTO`), the larger cultural area it is part of (`PARTOF`), and how precise its border is (`BORDERPRECISION`, 1 to 3). Shapes with no name are land that no state or people on that map covers.

**Until 1885:** [Cliopatria](https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria), from the Seshat Global History Databank (Bennett and others, 2025), version 0.2.1. It is one GeoJSON file of about 14,000 rows, each a polity's shape for a span of years (`FromYear` to `ToYear`, inclusive), with its `Name`, its `Area`, its `Wikipedia` and `Wikidata` titles, and, for a polity that belongs to a larger one, the group in brackets it is a member of (`MemberOf`, such as "(Spanish Empire)" or "(Holy Roman Empire)"). Rows whose name is in brackets are those groups, or relations between polities ("(Vassalage of … to …)", "(Allegiance of … to …)", "(Personal union of … with …)"). Cliopatria covers the world from 3400 BC to 2024, and CHRONIA uses it for every year to 1885. From 1200 it records a change somewhere on Earth every two and a half years on average, against historical-basemaps' 16 maps in those seven centuries. It grows thinner the further back it goes: it has a few states in Egypt, Mesopotamia, Elam, and the Indus valley in 3000 BC, about 30 in 1500 BC, 60 in 500 BC, and 70 in AD 300, against several hundred after 1200, and outside Eurasia and North Africa it has little before AD 500, so more of the map there comes from the snapshots.

What Cliopatria has, set against the other two sources:

- **Detail:** it divides what historical-basemaps draws as one shape. In 1650, the Holy Roman Empire is Brandenburg, Saxony, Bavaria, the Habsburg lands, and the rest; Hungary is split between the Habsburgs, the Ottomans, and Transylvania.
- **Coverage:** it records states only, no peoples or cultures, and leaves much of the Americas, Africa, Siberia, and Australia blank before colonial rule. In 1700 it covers 45% of the land outside Antarctica, against 71% for historical-basemaps' map with its peoples.
- **Coasts:** its coastlines are coarse, with steps several kilometres long. The page clips every shape to Natural Earth's land, so this shows only where a shape stops short of the coast.
- **Colonies:** a colonial empire is often one shape for all its colonies, such as "Spanish Empire" or "British Colonial Empire", a member of a group with the metropolis ("Kingdom of Spain").

The download is a zip of the GeoJSON in the repository, or on Zenodo (record 13363121).

**From 1886:** [CShapes 2.0](https://icr.ethz.ch/data/cshapes/) (Schvitz, Girardin, Rüegger, Weidmann, Cederman, and Gleditsch, 2022), from 1 January 1886 to 31 December 2019. It records every independent state and dependent territory as a series of spans, each with its own shape, start and end dates, status (independent, colony, protectorate, mandate, or occupied), owner, and capital. The builder reads the Gleditsch and Ward edition, `cshapes_2_gw.topojson`, and takes a few corrections from the Correlates of War edition, `cshapes_2_cow.topojson`. Both ship inside the CShapes R package. It is easiest to get from the CRAN mirror on GitHub, because the ETH site can't be reached from Claude's sessions.

**Small territories:** [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/)'s 10 m states and provinces (`ne_10m_admin_1_states_provinces.geojson`, public domain), for the territories that the other sources leave out (see [Small territories](#small-territories)). UCHRONIA's `provinces.json` comes from the same source, but simplified so far that Monaco, Gibraltar, Macau, and Vatican City have no area, so the builder reads the full file.

**Capitals from 1400 to 1885:** [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/)'s capital statements (P36), with their start and end qualifiers (P580 and P582), ranks, and the capitals' coordinates (P625) and inception (P571), and whether the state has a dissolution date (P576). `tools/fetch-capitals.mjs` reads the Wikipedia titles of the states and their dependencies in `data/eras.json` from 1400 to 1885, turns them into Wikidata ids through the Wikipedia API, following redirects, and asks the Wikidata query service for the rest. It writes `tools/capitals-wikidata.json`: `titles` (Wikipedia title to Wikidata id), `live` (the ids with no dissolution date), `since` (the founding year of each of those, P571), and `caps`, for each id a list of `[capital, longitude, latitude, start year, end year, preferred, the capital's inception]`. Neither site can be reached from Claude's sessions, so the file in the repository was fetched through a browser on Noah's computer, with the same queries. Run `node chronia/tools/fetch-capitals.mjs` to fetch it again, then rebuild.

### Building

```
git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/aourednik/historical-basemaps.git
git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/cran/cshapes.git
git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria.git
xz -dk cshapes/inst/extdata/cshapes_2_gw.topojson.xz cshapes/inst/extdata/cshapes_2_cow.topojson.xz
unzip cliopatria/cliopatria.geojson.zip -d cliopatria
curl -LO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/master/geojson/ne_10m_admin_1_states_provinces.geojson
npm i topojson-server topojson-client topojson-simplify d3-geo polygon-clipping @turf/buffer
node --max-old-space-size=6500 chronia/tools/build-eras.mjs node_modules historical-basemaps/geojson "" cshapes/inst/extdata/cshapes_2_gw.topojson cliopatria/cliopatria_polities_only_v021.geojson ne_10m_admin_1_states_provinces.geojson
```

The builder's arguments are, in order: the npm modules; historical-basemaps' `geojson` folder; UCHRONIA's `provinces.json`, where an empty string keeps the default, `../../uchronia/data/provinces.json` from the tools folder; CShapes' Gleditsch and Ward file; Cliopatria's GeoJSON (the file inside the zip is named for its version); and Natural Earth's 10 m states and provinces. The builder reads `cshapes_2_cow.topojson` from beside the Gleditsch and Ward file, for the corrections in `CS_FROM_COW` (see [From 1886](#from-1886)). Without it, the build says so and leaves them out.

The build takes about 35 minutes and needs about 6 GB of memory, so the command raises Node's limit. It writes `data/eras.json`, `data/dated/`, `data/modern.json`, and `data/land.json`, and `data/eras/` for any snapshot map still shown as a map before `DATED_FROM` (none now), but doesn't clear `data/`, so when files are no longer written, delete them first (or, where files can't be deleted, move them to `_to_delete/`). Keep `data/eras.json`, because the builder reads it to keep the colours (see step 6). When a map's data is wrong, fix it in the builder's tables, so that a rebuild keeps the fix. The cities, the first-level divisions, and the dated geography have builders of their own (see [Cities](#cities), [First-level divisions](#first-level-divisions), and [Geography over time](#geography-over-time)).

What the builder does to each map:

1. **Cleaning:**
   - `RENAME` merges names that mean the same state. For example, the 1815 map has both "United Kingdom" and "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland". It also repairs the 1878 map's "M?ori", whose macron the source lost, as Maori (written Māori in the yearly data; see step 8 of [Until 1885](#until-1885)).
   - `RULER_FIX` sets the ruler of dependencies that the source lists as independent, by name and years. Examples are the Philippines under Spain until 1898 and the United States after, Ceylon and the Cape Colony under Britain, and Kamerun and Togoland under Germany.
   - `RULER_ALIAS` and `LINEAGE` resolve a ruler named differently from any state on the same map. In 1914, for example, the British Raj is held by "United Kingdom", while the state on the map is "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland".
   - A ruler equal to the state itself, or written as a note in brackets, is dropped.
2. **Merging:** all shapes with the same name become one state, so that it is one entry and one click.
3. **Peoples:** a state is marked as a people or culture rather than a state when:
   - its name matches `PEOPLES` (hunter-gatherers, farmers, pastoralists, "cultures", Bantu, Khoisan, and so on); or
   - it is one of the Aboriginal nations on maps before 1900. This is detected as a shape in Australia with no ruler whose name is not a colony's.
   - it is one of the nations of the map of 1492 in the Americas, which draws Native Land's present-day nations (from 1446 to 1499, a shape in the Americas with no ruler), unless it is one of the states in `NATIVE_LAND_STATES` (the Inca Empire, the Aztec Triple Alliance, the Purépecha Empire, the Chimú, the Mayan city-states, the Mixtec and Zapotec city-states, the Haudenosaunee, the Muisca, the Itza, Tenochtitlan, Texcoco, and Tlaxcala). The same nations on the later maps are in `PEOPLES` by name. As peoples they give way to the states they overlap, so the Inca and the Aztecs get back the land the nations' shapes took from them.

   Peoples are drawn uncoloured with italic names, and their borders are dotted. A people whose name says it lived by hunting, gathering, foraging, or fishing (`BANDS`) is a band: its people value is 2 rather than 1, and it is drawn with no border at all, only its name, fainter, since such groups ranged over land rather than holding it.
4. **Two levels of detail:**
   - `hi`: simplified to about 1.6 km and quantized to 200,000 steps;
   - `lo`: simplified to about 12 km and quantized to 20,000 steps.

   Before simplifying, `smoothTopo` smooths stair-stepped edges, left where a source was traced from a grid of cells. It works on the shared arcs, so two neighbours still meet exactly. An arc is smoothed when at least 30% of its length is in segments that run exactly north–south or east–west: each run of short segments (under 0.5°) is replaced by its midpoints, keeping the ends of long straight segments in place, and then cut twice at the corners (Chaikin's method), with the arc's ends fixed. 20,397 arcs are smoothed. A border that runs along a parallel or a meridian for a long way, as many colonial borders do, keeps its straight line. The shapes from 1886, from CShapes, are not smoothed (`topoFor`'s last argument).

   Both are TopoJSON with one object, `p`, whose geometries are in the same order as `rows`. Simplifying and quantizing can turn a small polygon inside out, so that d3 takes it for the whole globe less the polygon, which confuses hovering and bounds. `topoFor` therefore ends with `outward()`, which reverses any polygon whose area comes out over half the globe, so the files have none (an earlier build had 1,170, mostly small pieces at the `lo` level).
5. **Labels:** for each state, the builder computes:
   - the pole of inaccessibility of its largest piece, with the radius of the largest circle inside it;
   - the room along a principal axis, with the axis angle when it lies between 12° and 40°;
   - its area.

   States over 1.5 million km² also get extra spots, one per 30° band of longitude that holds a large part of them. Each extra spot's room is measured along its own parallel across the whole state. The page lays out a name on the ground starting from the pole and these spots (see [Names of states and peoples](#names-of-states-and-peoples)), and sizes it by the area. It no longer uses the room and the angle.
6. **Colour:** every state gets one of the ten Antique colours, and keeps it across all maps:
   - States in a `LINEAGE` (England through the United Kingdom, Muscovy through the Russian Federation, the Chinese dynasties, the French monarchies and republics, and others) share one colour. The lineages include Cliopatria's names, such as the Bourbon Kingdom of France, the Second French Empire, and the French Third Republic.
   - A state that ends where another begins, in the same year or within a year, shares its colour with it when each covers at least 60% of the other, measured on a 0.25° grid. This joins 73 pairs that the lineages miss, such as Cliopatria's last span of a state in 1885 and its CShapes successor in 1886. A dependency is not joined this way, at either end, since it is shown in its ruler's colour: otherwise the Funj Sultanate, through the Egyptian Sudan, took the colour of Egypt, its neighbour. `NO_LINK` lists pairs that are not joined: Czechoslovakia and the Czech Republic, since the Czech Republic's base colour is Romania's, and Czechoslovakia bordered Romania until 1945 in CShapes, so it takes a colour of its own. `COLOUR_FROM` then gives a state another's colour from a date on: Czechoslovakia takes the Czech Republic's from 8 May 1945, once Ruthenia has gone to the Soviet Union and it no longer touches Romania. The date goes into the state's `polities` entry.
   - A dependency takes its ruler's colour.
   - Colours are chosen greedily, keeping neighbours apart round the colour wheel. Neighbours are the shapes that touch on any map, plus labels close across water on the snapshot maps. Until 1885, two states that touch count as neighbours in proportion to the years they touch, in full from 30 years (`TOUCH_YEARS`).
   - The builder reads the existing `data/eras.json`, when there is one, and keeps each state's earlier colour, matched by name (a name from 1886 first, and for a renamed state its old name). The states that had a colour are coloured first, largest first, and each keeps its earlier colour unless a state it touches already has that colour; then the new states are fitted round them. In the fitting, each change from the earlier colour costs 30 (`KEEP_COST`), against 100 for a neighbour in the same colour, 6 for a neighbour one step round the colour wheel, and 1 for one two steps round. A neighbour counts once however many maps the two touch on, so long borders don't push a state off its colour. So a build that reads its own output keeps every colour: on a second build all 2,517 colour keys keep theirs, and `eras.json`, `modern.json`, and every file in `data/dated/` come out byte for byte the same. Against the colours before the naming pass, 2,635 of the 2,711 names that had a colour and still exist kept it, and 76 changed: 24 of them are now always under a ruler, so they are shown in its colour as before, and 52 are seen in a new colour, among them Egypt, Luxembourg, Bolivia, and Indonesia after 1886. 58 names are gone, renamed. A first build, with no `eras.json`, chooses the colours afresh and warns.
   - **Base colours:** `BASE_COLOURS` gives 196 states of 2019, by name, the colours of the map Noah coloured in UCHRONIA and saved on 27 October 2026, which UCHRONIA's `BASE_COLORS` gives the same countries (Canada pale lime, the United States periwinkle, Brazil and Russia mint, China salmon, India pale orange, and so on; it replaced an earlier reference map read off a screenshot). They override the earlier colour, are placed first, and are never moved by the greedy pass or the repair, so the map of 2019 matches his map exactly and each lineage takes its modern state's colour back through time (the Kingdom of France the purple of France). Noah chose this over keeping every pair of neighbours apart, so where two of these lineages touched in the past in one colour, they stay in one colour (see [Known limits](#known-limits)).
   - **East, South, and Southeast Asia** (the box from 60° to 150° E and 15° S to 52° N, `SPREAD`): there, states whose labels are within 1,500 km of each other, beyond their own size, also count as weak neighbours (0.06). Before Cliopatria, China was red, and Japan, Korea, Mongolia, Burma, the Philippines, and the United Kingdom's colonies were nearly all one blue, so that from 1900 to 1930 a quarter of the region was blue and half red. Now Japan, Korea, Mongolia, Burma, Siam, and the Philippines each have their own colour, and India after 1947 no longer shares the United Kingdom's. The cause was partly the lineages: "British Empire", which Cliopatria uses for the East India Company's India, was in England's lineage, so it joined the United Kingdom to India; it is no longer there. The first build with Cliopatria let the states of the region change their colour for less, once, to break up the clusters; that is no longer needed, since their colours are now in `eras.json`.
   - **Repair:** the greedy pass can leave touching states in one colour: a state whose earlier colour a neighbour took first, and new states fitted in an order that left them no free colour. After it, the builder takes each pair of touching states in one colour and recolours the side with fewer names, to the colour closest to its old one that none of its neighbours uses. When every colour is taken round both sides, it moves one of the neighbours instead, where that neighbour has a free colour of its own. Here, touching means on any map (or, until 1885, in any year), and not across water.
   - **Colours by date:** the base colours leave some touching pairs in one colour (see below), mostly a colony in its ruler's colour beside a neighbour of the same base colour: Portuguese Mozambique and South Africa, British Sierra Leone and Liberia, French Algeria and Morocco or Niger, British Gambia and Senegal. For these, `windowColours` takes each pair of touching spans that are in one colour at the same time, and gives one side another colour for just those years or days, then its own colour again: the side that is independent rather than a dependency, or else the smaller. The colour is chosen to keep clear of every neighbour of that state in those years, a step or more round the colour wheel where it can, and as close as it can to its own colour. A state's stretches less than ten years apart (3,650 days from 1886) are joined into one, with its colour chosen again for the whole of it, so a state doesn't change colour every few years; the whole time the two touch is checked, at its start, middle, and end, and the pass runs twice. A state whose largest span is over 3 million km² (`WINDOW_MAX_KM2`) never changes colour, so China beside British Hong Kong, and the Qing dynasty beside British territory, still share a colour, as before; and two states of one lineage, such as Soviet Russia and the Russian Empire in 1918, are left alone. The steps go into the state's `polities` entry, `[[first day, colour], …]` from 1886 and `[[first year, colour], …]` until 1885, and the page's `polityColor(id, day, year)` picks the colour for the day or year on screen; the snapshots of the map are cached with the number of colour steps passed, so a colour changes on its day even when no border does. South Africa, for example, is drawn in another colour until Mozambique's independence in 1975, then in its own. The build prints how many such stretches it made.
   - The build prints how many touching pairs shared a colour after the greedy pass, how many colour keys the repair recoloured, and how many touching pairs still share a colour, with their names. At present 41 pairs share a colour after the greedy pass, the repair recolours 23 keys, and 20 pairs are left, nearly all of them between two lineages with base colours. Taking the yearly data back to 1200 gave about 30 states a new colour, among them the Golden Horde, the Ilkhanate, the Novgorod Republic, the Sultanate of Delhi, and Korea's line (Goryeo, Joseon, and Korea to 1910), because they now touch neighbours in the 13th and 14th centuries that they did not touch on the snapshots. Taking it back to 3400 BC gave about 70 states of 1200 to 1885 a new colour, among them the Byzantine Empire (with the Roman Empire, its lineage), the Kingdom of Hungary, the Crown of Aragon, and the Ayyubids, and two of today's, Georgia and Andorra, for the same reason.
7. **Wikipedia titles:** `WIKI` and `WIKI_BY_YEAR` give an article title where the plain name would find the wrong article, such as Han → Han dynasty, or France from 843 to 1792 → Kingdom of France.

### Until 1885

What the builder does with Cliopatria:

1. **Rows:** it keeps the rows of states, not the groups and relations in brackets, from its first year, 3400 BC (`DATED_FROM`). Consecutive rows of the same polity with the same shape are joined into one span. The microstates and the Isle of Man are left out (`CLIO_DROP`), because the small territories table draws them (see [Small territories](#small-territories)).
2. **Names:**
   - `CLIO_NAME` corrects names that are wrong or awkward: "Kingdom of Monaco" is the Principality of Monaco, "People's Republic of Zanzibar" (1856 to 1889) the Sultanate of Zanzibar, "Hashemite Arab Federation" (1415 to 1439, a name from 1958) Arab Iraq, "Napoleonic Batavia Republic" the Dutch Cape Colony, "Livonian Conference" the Livonian Confederation, "Free Orange State" the Orange Free State, "Holy Roman Empire Minor States" the Holy Roman Empire, and "United States of America" the United States, as from 1886.
   - Brackets are dropped ("Kingdom of Naples (Napoleonic)" is the Kingdom of Naples), and "Dynasty" at the end of a name is written "dynasty", as Wikipedia does ("Ming dynasty").
   - `CLIO_RENAME` renames by year: Cliopatria keeps "Kingdom of Great Britain" until 1889, so from 1801 it is the United Kingdom.
3. **Rulers and parts:** a member of a group is held by the group's head: the member with the group's own name ("Kingdom of France" in "(Kingdom of France)"), or the head in `CLIO_HEAD` where the group is named for its colonies: "(Spanish Empire)" is headed by the Kingdom of Spain, "(Portuguese Empire)" by the Kingdom of Portugal, and "(British Empire)" by the Kingdom of Great Britain. So the colonies of an empire, and the fiefs of a kingdom (Foix, Brittany, Burgundy), take its colour. The groups in `CLIO_PART` are not rulers: a member of "(Holy Roman Empire)", "(Kalmar Union)", "(Delhi Sultanate)", or "(Polish-Lithuania Kingdom)" is part of it, as the card says. A "(Vassalage of … to …)" relation makes the overlord the vassal's ruler for its years. Allegiances and personal unions are not shown as rule.
4. **Wikipedia:** each row's own `Wikipedia` title is used for its years. `CLIO_WIKI` replaces the few that are wrong (the Kingdom of Pajana's is "Lontara script", the Livonian Confederation's "Livonian language", Sambaliung's a link to the Indonesian Wikipedia).
5. **Filling from historical-basemaps:** Cliopatria has no peoples and leaves much land blank, so each of historical-basemaps' maps from 3000 BC to 1880 fills the land that Cliopatria leaves blank in the years nearest to it: from halfway after the map before to halfway before the map after (the 1815 map from 1797 to 1846, the map of AD 300 from 250 to 349, for example), and the first map, of 3000 BC, from 3400 BC. For each shape on the map and each stretch of years in which the Cliopatria shapes around it stay the same, the builder cuts those shapes out of it.
   - What is left is kept in pieces at least 1,500 km² in area and 25 km wide on average, or 60% of the whole shape, so that the slivers between two sources' borders and coasts are dropped. For a shape that is itself small or narrow, such as the Gambia, the limits shrink with it: 30% of its area, and 60% of its own average width.
   - When the shape is the same polity as a Cliopatria one around it, the pieces are joined to the Cliopatria polity's shape, year by year. It is the same polity when its name is in the same lineage, when the two names share their distinctive words ("Algiers" and "Regency of Algiers", "Hafsid Caliphate" and "Hafsid dynasty", leaving out words such as kingdom, empire, dynasty, and of, listed in `NAME_FILLER`), or when one Cliopatria polity covers at least half of the shape. So the United States of 1815 includes the Louisiana Purchase, which Cliopatria leaves out, and Algiers has one name and one border, not two. Pieces joined this way are kept down to 20 km² and 4 km wide (`OWN_KM2`, `OWN_WIDTH_KM`), since they only fill out the polity's own coast and edges: this puts Naples, which Cliopatria's coarse coast leaves out, in the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies. In all, 2,479 pieces are joined to Cliopatria spans.
   - Otherwise the pieces keep their historical-basemaps name, ruler, and kind (9,308 spans), so the Siberians, the Plains bison hunters, and the Aboriginal nations stay on the map as peoples.
   - To keep the build quick, every shape is first drawn into a grid of 0.25° cells, and a shape is cut only by the Cliopatria shapes whose cells meet its own. A shape at least 97% covered is dropped without cutting, unless what is left of it comes to 20,000 km² or more (`BIG_REST_KM2`): then it is cut, and only its pieces of at least 5,000 km² and 40 km across are kept (`bigPiece`). So a large state keeps a large piece that the other source leaves out, such as Novaya Zemlya and the Chukchi Peninsula east of 180° for the Russian Empire, without keeping the slivers along its borders.
6. **Overlaps in Cliopatria:** where two Cliopatria shapes overlap in the same years, the larger loses the overlap and the smaller keeps it (965 pairs). So the Duchy of Lorraine is not under the Kingdom of France, and in 1783 the Kingdom of Naples is not under the Habsburg Monarchy, whose shape Cliopatria draws over it.
7. **Member states:** Cliopatria often draws one shape where historical-basemaps draws several states: an umbrella over its members (the German Confederation over Prussia, Hanover, and Saxony in 1815; the Holy Roman Empire's minor states; the Confederation of the Rhine), or a state that swallows its neighbours or dependencies (the Austrian Empire over Tuscany in 1815; the Kingdom of Spain over Naples, Sicily, Sardinia, and Milan in 1700; the Kingdom of England over Scotland in 1700; the East India Company's "British Empire" over Mysore, Oudh, and Ceylon). The builder then draws the smaller state out of the larger one, and shows it as a polity of its own:
   - A state on a historical-basemaps map is drawn out of a Cliopatria shape when at least half of it lies in that shape and it is not the same polity (by the name test above). If the shape is an umbrella, named in `UMBRELLA` (confederation, Holy Roman Empire, union, confederacy, league, Haudenosaunee), the state may be up to 95% of it. Otherwise it may be at most half of it, and if it is more than a quarter of it, it must not hold the larger shape's centre, so that a state's own core under another name (Annam in the Nguyễn state, Oman under the Yaʿrubids) is left alone.
   - The larger shape loses the state's shape for those years; the state keeps the part of its own shape that lies in the larger one or in blank land. What is left of the larger shape loses its slivers, pieces under 300 km² or 8 km wide that are less than a tenth of it (`SLIVER_KM2`, `SLIVER_WIDTH_KM`), which are left where the two sources' borders and coasts differ. Pieces of the larger shape that are left mostly inside the state's area (the Hebrides, Orkney, and the Highland coasts that Cliopatria gives the Kingdom of England in 1700) go to the state.
   - An umbrella's members get the umbrella as their part ("Part of German Confederation" on the card). Other states keep their ruler from historical-basemaps, matched to a Cliopatria name in force (Lombardy is held by the Austrian Empire, Ceylon by the United Kingdom), and are independent when it gives none (Tuscany, Scotland).
   - The state takes the name of a Cliopatria polity of the same name within 60 years (`TWIN_YEARS`), if there is one, so Hanover is the Kingdom of Hanover and Saxony the Kingdom of Saxony.
   - Because such states change less often than the umbrella's outline, a map's states are drawn out until the next map, not only in the years nearest to it, while the larger shape is the one that held the state in the map's own year; an umbrella new since that year counts for 15 years either side (`UMBRELLA_YEARS`). So Prussia, Hanover, and Saxony are drawn out of the German Confederation from 1815 to 1877, but Lombardy is not drawn out of the Kingdom of Sardinia after 1859.
   - `HB_STALE` gives the last year of states that historical-basemaps draws after their end: New Amsterdam (1664), Dutch Brazil (1654), Dutch Formosa (1662), the Post-Ming Warlords (1681), and the Guanches (1496). After that year, their land goes to the Cliopatria state that covers most of their shape in each year (the Crown of Castile and the Spanish Empire for the Guanches, the English colonies for New Amsterdam, Portugal for Dutch Brazil, Tungning and then the Qing for Dutch Formosa). It also ends historical-basemaps' Greece in 1831, since its map of 1880 draws Albania as Greece.
   - This draws 3,249 spans of member states out of 970 Cliopatria spans.
8. **Names:** both sources name some states wrongly for the year: an earlier or later regime ("First Hellenic Republic" to 1885, "Denmark-Norway" after 1814, the "Golden Horde" in 1600), a people for a state ("Serbs" for Serbia), source codes ("Algeria (FR)", "Queensland (UK)"), modern names ("Papua New Guinea" in 1450, "United States of America" for the colonies in 1750), misspellings ("Britany", "Sultinate of Zanzibar"), or two names for one state ("Chimu Empire" and "Chimú Empire", "Scotland" and "Kingdom of Scotland"). After the fill, the builder fixes them in three tables, splitting a span where the name changes within it:
   - `REGIMES` gives each name in a state's line of regimes the regime of the year, for names, rulers, and parts alike: France from the Kingdom of France to the Third Republic; England, the Commonwealth, Great Britain, and the United Kingdom; the Dutch Republic, the Batavian Republic, the Kingdom of Holland, and the Netherlands; Spain and its First Republic; Portugal; the Habsburg Monarchy, the Austrian Empire, and Austria-Hungary; Muscovy and Russia; Denmark-Norway and Denmark; and Sweden. A ruler written "France", "UK", "Spain", "Netherlands Antilles", or "British Colonial Empire" becomes the regime of the year, so French colonies in 1850 are held by the Second Republic and British ones in 1750 by the Kingdom of Great Britain.
   - `NAME_FIX` renames a name for a range of years, optionally only where the shape's centre lies in a box, and can set its ruler, its Wikipedia title, and whether it is a people. Where a span has pieces both in and out of a box (a Portuguese Empire span with Brazil and Mozambique), it is parted first. The dates are the usual ones in Wikipedia's article for each state (see [Names fixed](#names-fixed)).
   - `RULER_DROP` drops rulers that no longer existed (the Mongol Empire after 1368).
   - A `NAME_FIX` entry with `drop` removes a name for its years, where a source keeps a state long after its end and there is no successor to give the land to (the Buyids, the Ghaznavids, Kediri from 1240 to 1499).
   - `CARVE` splits a shape that a source draws over two states: the part inside a reference shape keeps the name, and the rest takes another. Cliopatria draws the Archduchy of Austria over Hungary from 1453 to 1458, under Ladislaus the Posthumous, who held both; the part outside Austria's shape of 1452 is the Kingdom of Hungary.
   - `APPROX` lists the states and peoples whose borders the sources only guess at (the steppe khanates, the Sahel, Tibet, the Americas before Europeans, Tonga, the Māori); their precision is set to 1, so their borders are dotted and the card calls them approximate.

   Then spans of one state with one name and ruler that touch in the same years are joined into one, so a state has one label, not one per piece (Scotland and Sardinia in 1700 had two). A renamed state keeps its earlier colour (see step 6 of [Building](#building)).

   Overlaps left by all of this are settled at the end of the build (see [Shapes over each other](#shapes-over-each-other)), and the borders are then given the lines of 1886 where they differ from them only by Cliopatria's steps (see [Meeting the lines of 1886](#meeting-the-lines-of-1886)).
9. **Small territories:** see below.
10. **Capitals:** from 1400, each span takes its capital, year by year, from `tools/capitals-wikidata.json` (see [The sources](#the-sources-and-how-they-are-combined)), matched by the span's Wikipedia title. In a year:
    - the statements whose start and end take in the year count, and of those the one that starts latest, then the preferred one, then the one that ends latest, so the Ottoman Empire's capital is Bursa, then Edirne from 1365, then Constantinople from 1453;
    - if no dated statement takes in the year, the statements with no dates count, except, for a state that still exists, before the state's own founding or the capital's, so Senegal's Dakar is not given to the Senegal of 1700, nor Myanmar's Naypyidaw to Burma;
    - a statement with no start is skipped before its capital was founded, where Wikidata dates that before 1886, so the Ethiopian Empire has no capital before Gondar's founding in 1635;
    - of several, the preferred one is taken; capitals within 25 km of each other count as one place (London, the City of London, and Westminster), named by the shortest name; otherwise the year has no capital, so a state whose capital moved with no dates in Wikidata, such as the Mughal Empire between Delhi and Lahore outside its dated stretches, has none;
    - a capital more than about 30 km outside the span's land (`CAP_NEAR_DEG`, 0.3°) is left out, which drops the capitals of empires given to their colonies, such as London for British North America;
    - `CAP_SKIP` leaves out the Holy Roman Empire, whose statements are the seats of its courts and diet rather than a capital, and `CAP_DROP` drops single statements: Székesfehérvár for the Kingdom of Hungary, the coronation city, so Buda is its capital, Rabat for Morocco, the capital only from 1912, and Brades for Montserrat, the capital only from 1998;
    - `CAP_NAME` gives the city's own name where Wikidata's label is for a district, a heritage site, or a later spelling (Coop of Brussels, Historic City of Ayutthaya, Toledo city, Kolkata as Calcutta, Ahilyanagar as Ahmednagar). On the page, a capital also takes the names by date of the city at its place, as from 1886.

    This gives a capital to 4,496 spans. A span's capitals are written in the same form as from 1886 (see [From 1886](#from-1886)): the capital of its last years, with the earlier ones as a list ending on 31 December of their last year.
11. **Files:** the spans, 26,329 in all, are written as one TopoJSON per century, `data/dated/bc3400.json` to `1800.json`, 53 files, at the same two levels of detail as the snapshots. A span that crosses into the next century is in both files. Labels are computed per span, as for the snapshots, and spans with the same shape share one.
12. **Changes:** every year a span starts, or the year after one ends, is a change: 653 years from 3400 BC to 1885, 363 of them before 1200.

A row in a file in `data/dated/` is `[id, ruler id, first year, last year, status, capital, label, people, part-of id, precision]`, where the capital is `null` or as in `modern.json` (see [From 1886](#from-1886)), with a name of `null` for years with no capital, and the status is 0 for a state, 1 for a colony, 2 for a protectorate, 4 for an occupied territory, and 5 for another dependency.

### The Holy Roman Empire

The Empire was a state of states: hundreds of principalities, bishoprics, and free cities, each with its own ruler, under an emperor and an imperial diet. Cliopatria marks only some of them as its members, and historical-basemaps draws the Empire as one shape with no members. So the builder decides membership by the Empire's outline, and the page draws the Empire like the United States with its states: one colour, one name over the whole, and a fine broken line between the members, each of which keeps its own name, ruler, and card.

- **The outline:** historical-basemaps' shape of the Holy Roman Empire on its maps of 1279 (used for 1200 to 1299, since its map of 1200 leaves out Bohemia and Brandenburg), 1300, 1400, 1492, 1500, 1530, 1600, and 1650, and the two of 1650 and 1700 together from 1700 until the Empire ended in 1806 (`GROUPS` in the builder). These follow the usual atlas outline: imperial Italy until 1530, the Swiss Confederation until 1492, the Low Countries until 1648, Holstein but not Schleswig, and the Habsburgs' Austria and Bohemia but not Hungary.
- **Members:** after the overlaps are settled, every span of the years of an outline that lies at least 80% inside it is a member (`part` is the Holy Roman Empire). One that lies partly inside, by at least 3,000 km², or by 500 km² and a tenth of its area, is cut along the outline into a member and the rest: the Habsburg Monarchy into Austria and Bohemia and the Hungarian lands, Brandenburg-Prussia into Brandenburg and Prussia, the Swedish Empire's Pomerania and Bremen-Verden from Sweden. Cliopatria draws Saxony inside the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth from 1700 to 1762, the years of the personal union under Augustus II and Augustus III; `NAME_FIX` makes it the Electorate of Saxony again, so it is a member. Peoples, disputed land, small territories, and states that were never members are left out (`never`: France, Venice, the Papal States, Hungary, Poland and Lithuania, the Teutonic Order's Prussia, and the Ottomans). A span that Cliopatria marks as a member but lies outside the outline is no longer one.
- **The Empire's name:** for each century's file, the builder joins the members of each year into one shape with `topojson.merge` and computes a label for it, as for any state. The file's `groups` lists `[group id, first year, last year, [row indexes], label]` for each stretch of years in which the members are the same.
- **On the page:** a member takes the Empire's colour, whatever its ruler (Swedish Pomerania is in the Empire's colour, held by Sweden). Two members, or a member and the Empire's own remaining land, meet along a broken line in border ink, 0.7 to 1 px wide, dashes of 4.2 widths and gaps of 2.6, so the members read like the states of a country. The Empire's name is laid out over the merged shape first, before the names of the members, and the hover names a member's place ("in the Holy Roman Empire").

The build reports the members and the cuts: at present 464 spans are inside the Empire and 378 are cut at its border.

### The Roman provinces

From AD 106 to 117 the Roman Empire is drawn like the Holy Roman Empire: one colour and one name, with its provinces inside, divided by fine broken lines, each with its own name and card.

- **The source:** the provinces of the [Digital Atlas of the Roman Empire](https://dare.ht.lu.se/) (DARE, Johan Åhlfeldt), as published in [klokantech/roman-empire](https://github.com/klokantech/roman-empire)'s `data/provinces.geojson`, under CC BY. Its map is of Trajan's empire: it has Dacia and Arabia Petraea, annexed in 106, and Armenia and Mesopotamia, held from 114 to 117, and still draws Galatia with Cappadocia and Epirus within Achaia. So the provinces are shown from 106, the year of Dacia and Arabia, to 117, the year of Trajan's death, after which Hadrian gave up Armenia and Mesopotamia; Armenia et Mesopotamia only from 114. Italy is shown in its eleven Augustan regions, which DARE draws as units.
- **The table:** `tools/roman-provinces.json` holds each province as `[name, Wikipedia title, first year, last year, polygons]`, with DARE's coordinates rounded to four decimals. The names are written out as atlases write them ("Gallia Lugdunensis" for DARE's "Lugdunensis", the regions by their names, such as "Latium et Campania" for "I").
- **The cut:** after the overlaps are settled and the Holy Roman Empire's members are drawn out, each span of the Roman Empire in those years is cut by the provinces (`PROVINCE_SETS` in the builder). Each province keeps the part of the span inside it, as a span named for the province with the Roman Empire as its part. What is left of the span outside every province, the slivers between DARE's coast and Cliopatria's, is given to the province whose cells meet it most (1,657 pieces), except pieces of at least 5,000 km² and 40 km across, which stay the Roman Empire's (3 spans). The build reports the counts.
- **On the page:** as for the Holy Roman Empire (see above): the provinces take the Empire's colour, its name is laid out over the whole, the provinces' names are smaller, and the hover names a province's place ("in the Roman Empire").

The provinces before 106 and after 117 are not drawn, since no open source maps them by date. The Ancient World Mapping Center's open data has the provincial boundaries of about AD 200 and after Diocletian as unnamed lines only, and was not used.

### Names fixed

`NAME_FIX` holds a few hundred renames; the full list, with years and regions, is in the builder. The main kinds, with examples:

- **Regimes by date:** "Serbs" is Revolutionary Serbia from 1804, the Principality of Serbia from 1815, and the Kingdom of Serbia from 1882; the First Hellenic Republic is the Kingdom of Greece from 1832; the Tokugawa Shogunate starts in 1603 and is the Empire of Japan from 1868; the Mughal Empire runs from 1526 to 1857; Brandenburg-Prussia is the Kingdom of Prussia from 1701; the Republic of Florence is the Duchy of Florence from 1532 and the Grand Duchy of Tuscany from 1569; Cliopatria's Kingdom of Hanover before 1814 is the Electorate.
- **Successors of a stale name:** Cliopatria's "Golden Horde" after 1502 is the Astrakhan Khanate, the Mongols, and Khalkha under the Qing; its Southern Ming after 1662 is the Kingdom of Tungning and the Three Feudatories, or the Qing; the Byzantine Empire after 1453 is the Despotate of the Morea and the Principality of Theodoro; the Sultanate of Malacca after 1511 is Portuguese, then Dutch Malacca, then the Straits Settlements.
- **Colonies by place:** Cliopatria's "British Empire" is British America in New England, British India in India, and British Guiana in Guiana; its overseas "Kingdom of Spain" is the Spanish Empire; "Luisiana" is Louisiana under France, then Spain, then the Louisiana Territory; "Quebec" is New France, the Province of Quebec, and Lower Canada; "British Africa" of 1885 is the Khedivate of Egypt under the United Kingdom.
- **Codes and spellings:** "Algeria (FR)" is French Algeria, and the "(UK)", "(Spain)", "(Netherlands)", and "(Danemark)" suffixes are dropped; Britany is the Duchy of Brittany, Chimu Empire the Chimú Empire, and Maori, Maoris, and M?ori are Māori.
- **Peoples:** "Papua New Guinea" before 1886 is the Papuans, and the Nogai Horde after 1634 the Nogais.
- **Before 1200:** historical-basemaps' names are merged with Cliopatria's for the same state, so a state has one name and one shape (the Mauryan Empire with the Maurya Empire, Parthia with the Parthian Empire, the Carthaginian Empire with Carthage, the Nabatean and Nabataean Kingdom, the four tetrarchs' domains of AD 300 with the Roman Empire, and about 30 more, in `RENAME` and `CLIO_NAME`); archaeological cultures and broad groups that the snapshots drew as states are peoples, under the names scholarship uses (the Andronovo, Afanasievo, Sintashta, and Bell Beaker cultures; the Celts, Illyrians, Scythians, and Tibeto-Burman peoples); and names out of their time are renamed or dropped: Egypt before 3000 BC is the Naqada culture, the Ur of 3400 to 1751 BC only the Third Dynasty of Ur from 2112 to 2004 BC, the "Zhoa" of 1750 BC the Zhou dynasty only from 1046 BC, Cliopatria's Later Zhou of the 8th to 3rd centuries BC Eastern Zhou, its Macedonian Empire before Alexander and after 318 BC the Kingdom of Macedonia, its Great Yuan Dayuan, its Kingdom of Italy before 774 the Kingdom of the Lombards, the Kingdom of David and Solomon of the snapshots, drawn from 1750 to 601 BC, is left out, Cliopatria's Kingdoms of Israel and Judah holding the land, as are their Hittites before 1650 BC, their Kush after AD 350, and their Sasanian dependencies after 651. Each change, with its sources, is in [LEDGER.md](LEDGER.md).
- **1200 to 1399:** the Mongols are a people until the khanate of 1206; the snapshots' Great Seljuk and Hamdanid names are the Khwarazmian Empire, the Sultanate of Rum, the Saltukids, the Eldiguzids, the Ayyubids, the Ilkhanate, and the Eretnids, by year; the Sultanate of Delhi is the Ghurids, then the Mamluk, Khalji, and Tughlaq dynasties; Tibet is under the Yuan from 1294 to 1353, then the Phagmodrupa; the Chola after 1279 is the Pandya, then Vijayanagara; Pagan is the Pagan Kingdom, then Myinsaing, Pinya, and Ava; Ceylon is Dambadeniya, Kurunegala, Gampola, and Kotte; "Ethiopia" is the Zagwe, then the Ethiopian Empire; the Kingdom of Alba is Scotland, the "Celtic kingdoms" Gaelic Ireland, and the "Viking settlements" the Lordship of Ireland, Norse Greenland, or Norway; and the Toltec, Huari, and Tiahuanaco, long gone by 1200, give way to city-states and chiefdoms. Each change, with its sources, is in [LEDGER.md](LEDGER.md).

`SMALL` lists territories that the sources leave blank or merge into a neighbour, with their shape from Natural Earth and their status by date, each from the territory's Wikipedia article. For each period it gives the status and a point in the ruler's capital, so the ruler is whoever holds that point at the time: the Isle of Man is held by the Kingdom of England, then Great Britain, then the United Kingdom.

- **Microstates:** Liechtenstein from 23 January 1719; Monaco from 1419 to 1792, under Sardinian protection from 30 May 1814 to 1860, and independent from 1861; San Marino from 1400, with Fiorentino, Montegiardino, and Serravalle from 1463 and Faetano from 1464; Andorra from 1400, except when France annexed it in 1812 and 1813; Vatican City from 7 June 1929.

Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Greenland, Svalbard, Bermuda, the Kurils, and the islands and the Hejaz above are only laid on land that is blank, so where a source has them, the source wins. Those under 3,000 km² are not simplified, so they keep their outline at every level of detail. There are 92 spans of them from 1400 to 1885, and 95 from 1886.

### From 1886

What the builder does to CShapes:

1. **Names by date:** `CS_NAMES` gives each CShapes name its names over time, and a span that crosses a renaming is split in two with the same shape. For example, Russia (Soviet Union) becomes the Russian Empire, then Soviet Russia from 7 November 1917, the Soviet Union from 30 December 1922, and Russia from 26 December 1991. The table holds about 70 such names, from Ceylon to Sri Lanka to Upper Volta and Burkina Faso. Other names lose their brackets.
2. **Corrections:** the Gleditsch and Ward edition keeps a state in being while it is occupied or annexed in war, so it has no Anschluss. `CS_FROM_COW` takes a country's spans for a stretch of time from CShapes' own Correlates of War edition instead, which has the same shapes. It is used for Germany and Austria from 14 March 1938 to 12 April 1945: Germany with Austria from 14 March 1938, and with the Sudetenland too from 30 September 1938, and Austria again from 13 April 1945. `CS_CARVE` lengthens a span whose end date is wrong and cuts its shape out of a neighbour's for the extra time. It is used for the Free City of Danzig, which both editions end on 31 August 1938 and add to Germany from 30 September 1938, a year early: Danzig now lasts until 31 August 1939, and Germany from 30 September 1938 to 31 August 1939 is drawn without it. Both tables take only shapes and dates that are already in CShapes. `CS_REDRAW` redraws the line between two CShapes states that CShapes draws in the wrong place, from Natural Earth's first-level units on each side: the two states' shapes are joined and split again along the units' shared boundary, so each keeps CShapes' coast and outer borders. Land that CShapes gives to either state but Natural Earth's units leave out (along coasts and outer borders, where the two sources' lines differ) goes, piece by piece, to the side whose units are nearer, within 25 km of the first state's units. It is used for the inner German border from 8 May 1945 to 2 October 1990: CShapes gives the Soviet zone and East Germany Wolfsburg, Helmstedt, and the western Harz, and they now follow the boundary of the five eastern states and Berlin, which is the line of 1945 to 1990 except for Amt Neuhaus (see the ledger).
3. **Owners:** a dependency's owner code is matched to the owner's name on the day the span starts. It becomes the ruler, so a colony takes its ruler's colour, as before 1886.
4. **Capitals:** `CAP_FIX` corrects spellings (Bukarest, Tirane, Masqat, and others), and the builder's small `CITY_NAMES` table gives a few capitals' names by date (St. Petersburg, Petrograd, and Leningrad; Tsaritsyn, Stalingrad, and Volgograd) and tidies others' spellings. On the page, a capital also takes the names by date of the city at its place (see [Cities](#cities)), so Harare shows as Salisbury in 1950. CShapes gives each span a single capital, and for some spans that is a later capital, so `CAP_BEFORE` lists earlier capitals by date:
   - Abuja: Lagos until 11 December 1991;
   - Dodoma: Dar es Salaam until 1996;
   - Lilongwe: Zomba until 1974;
   - Lusaka: Livingstone until 1935;
   - Gaborone: Mafeking until January 1965;
   - Nouakchott: Saint-Louis until 1957;
   - Kampala: Entebbe until independence in 1962;
   - Beijing: Peking until June 1928, Nanjing to November 1937, Chongqing to May 1946, and Nanjing again to September 1949.
5. **Filling from Cliopatria:** CShapes records only states and recognised dependencies. For each Cliopatria span from 1886, the builder cuts out the CShapes spans around it, day by day, and keeps the pieces that are left, with the same filter as before 1886, when at least half of the Cliopatria shape is left. A Cliopatria polity with the same name as a CShapes one, or in the same lineage, is left out, because CShapes is the authority for it, except those in `CLIO_TWIN`, which are kept under the CShapes name: the First Hellenic Republic's Macedonia from 30 May to 9 August 1913, between the treaties of London and Bucharest, is shown as Greece. `CLIO_MODERN_DROP` leaves out Cliopatria's British Africa, which spreads over the Sudan of the Mahdist State, and its New Hebrides, an independent state from 1890 there. This adds 91 spans, such as the Emirates of Nejd and Jabal Shammar in Arabia until 1921, the Mahdist State in the Sudan from 1890 to 1899, and the Sultanate of Hobyo. Cliopatria's changes are dated to 1 January of their year.
6. **Small territories:** the same table as before 1886, from its dates in days, cut out of the CShapes spans or laid on blank land. This adds 95 spans, among them Liechtenstein, Monaco, San Marino, Andorra, and Vatican City, the Isle of Man, Jersey, Guernsey, Gibraltar, Hong Kong, Macau, Greenland, the Faroe Islands, Bahrain before 1971, the Pacific and South Atlantic islands, and the Hejaz.
7. **Earlier shapes from later records:** CShapes starts some states and colonies years after they held their land, so the land is blank before. `CS_BACK` lays a CShapes record's first shape on the land still blank before it, under the names and statuses of the time: German East Africa from 1886 (CShapes starts it in 1891); the Mahdist State in the Sudan to 1 September 1898, then the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan; Ottoman Tripolitania and Ottoman Yemen, in the shapes of Italian Libya (1912) and North Yemen (1918), as part of the Ottoman Empire; Saudi Arabia's shape of 1932 as the Emirate of Nejd, the Emirate of Jabal Shammar from 24 January 1891, the Emirate of Nejd again from 15 January 1902, the Sultanate of Nejd from 22 August 1921, and the Kingdom of Hejaz and Nejd from 8 January 1926; the Aden Protectorate in the shapes of Aden and the East Aden Protectorate (1937); and the Trucial States to 7 March 1892. A piece whose name is a CShapes state's is added to that state's shape. This adds 35 spans. `CS_NAMES` names Tanzania German East Africa until 21 July 1920 and the United Arab Emirates the Trucial States until 1 December 1971.
8. **Filling from historical-basemaps:** each of its maps from 1880 fills the land still blank in the years nearest to it, as before 1886 (the 1880 map to 1889, the 1900 map from 1890 to 1906, the 1914 map from 1907 to 1916, and so on to the 2010 map from 2005). A piece of a shape whose land is mostly held by one span already (at least half of its 0.25° cells) is added to that span, if it is at least 5,000 km² and 40 km across, so the Ottoman Empire takes Libya's interior and much of the Syrian desert from the maps' Ottoman Empire; a shape mostly on blank land is kept under its own name, with the same filter as before 1886, such as the Sokoto Caliphate, the Tukular Caliphate, the Mossi States, the Lozi, and Kanem-Bornu before the colonial borders reach them, and islands such as Niue, Guam, and Palau. Peoples are left out. `HB_MODERN_DROP` leaves out names that are regions rather than states ("Arabia", "Arabia (Nejd)", "Mesopotamia (GB)", Antarctica) and those the table of small territories has (American Samoa, the New Hebrides), and `HB_MODERN_SKIP` keeps Svalbard blank until it became Norwegian. This adds 213 spans, and 57 pieces to spans already there.
9. **Disputed land:** `DISPUTES` lists the well-known disputes of the time, each with its shape from Natural Earth's disputed areas (`ne_10m_admin_0_disputed_areas.geojson`, read from beside the states and provinces file; without it, the build says so and leaves the disputes out), its dates, the state that holds it, if one does, and its claimants by date. Each period is cut out of the spans around it and becomes a span of its own, with status `disputed` and its claimants. The list: Jammu and Kashmir, Azad Kashmir, and Gilgit-Baltistan from 1947, and the Siachen Glacier from 1984 (India and Pakistan); Aksai Chin and Arunachal Pradesh (the North-East Frontier Agency until 1972) from 1959, and the Shaksam Valley from 1963 (China and India); Western Sahara from 1976 (Morocco, Mauritania until 1979, and the Sahrawi Republic); Somaliland from 1991; Guayana Esequiba from 1966 (Guyana and Venezuela); Crimea, and the Donetsk and Luhansk People's Republics, from 2014; the Golan Heights and the West Bank from 1967 (with Jordan until 1988, then Palestine); the Halaib Triangle from 1958 (Egypt and Sudan); the southern Kuril Islands from 1945 (the Soviet Union, then Russia, and Japan); Abyei from 2011 and the Ilemi Triangle from 1963; Northern Cyprus from 1974; Abkhazia, South Ossetia, and Transnistria from 1992 and 1993; and Kosovo from 2008. Taiwan is left out, since it has had its own government since 1949, and so is Artsakh, whose shape in Natural Earth is what was left of it after 2020, far smaller than what it held from 1994.
10. **One topology:** all the spans go into one TopoJSON, so the borders shared between spans are stored once, at two levels of detail: `lo` as the snapshots', and `hi` simplified to about 0.5 km and quantized to 500,000 steps (`CS_HI_KM` and `CS_HI_Q`), finer than the snapshots' because CShapes' own borders are finer. The finer `hi` makes `modern.json` 3.6 MB, up from 3.0 MB. Labels are computed per span, and spans with the same shape share a label.
11. **Colours:** each country code keeps one colour key throughout, taken from its first name: its lineage, or its historical-basemaps name if it has one. A state therefore keeps the colour it had in 1885 where the names match or where it succeeds a state of 1885 (see step 6 of [Building](#building)). Neighbours are spans that touch and overlap in time.
12. **Changes:** every day a span starts, or the day after one ends, goes into `eras.json` as a change, 468 in all, including the renamings from `CS_NAMES`.

A row in `modern.json` is `[id, owner id, first day, last day, status, capital, label]`, and a disputed span (status 6) adds an eighth item, the ids of its claimants; its owner is the state that holds it, or `-1`. A dated row can carry its claimants as an eleventh item in the same way. Days count from 1 January 1886, and a last day of `-1` means the span runs to the end of the data. A capital is `[name, lon, lat, names by day, earlier capitals]`, where the last two are optional: the fourth is `null` when a capital has earlier capitals but no names by day, and the fifth is a list of `[last day, name, longitude, latitude]` from `CAP_BEFORE`. The page's `capitalAt(cap, day)` picks the capital in force on the day, for the map and for the card.

### Cities

#### What the page shows

The page shows, at every date, the cities that existed then, ranked by their population at that date, not today's. Each city has a first year, a last year (or none, if it still exists), a series of populations by year, and, where it changed, a list of names by date.

On each date the page takes the cities that exist, estimates each one's population by interpolating between its two nearest figures (geometrically, so a city that grew from 100,000 to 400,000 over a century is 200,000 halfway), and sorts them. The rank gives the tier:

- the 68 largest: tier 1, from a globe radius of 330 px;
- the next 226: tier 2, from 480 px;
- the next 206: tier 3, from 800 px;
- the rest: tier 4, from 1,400 px.

The numbers are those of UCHRONIA's city tiers, so a map of 2019 is about as full as before. The ranking is kept for each date and redone only when the date changes, in about 1 ms. `data/cities-dated-minor.json` is loaded when the map first reaches tier 4.

Capitals are drawn with a ringed mark and upright names at 12.4 px. Tier 1 cities have a solid dot and upright names at 11.2 px, and tiers 2 to 4 are drawn as towns, with a smaller dot and upright names at 10.8 px. All are in a warm brown ink (`--ch-city`, and `--ch-town` for towns), with a thin, half-clear parchment halo (`--ch-city-halo`), so that they read as engraved over every colour and over the sea.

Capitals come from the border data, from 1886 and from 1400 to 1885 (see step 10 of [Until 1885](#until-1885)), and are drawn from a globe radius of 230 px. A city that is the capital being drawn is not drawn again as a city; the page finds it by its name (at the date) near the capital's position, so Brazzaville and Kinshasa, across the river from each other, stay apart. The capital's name is then taken from the city's names by date, so the capital and the card both say Peking in 1960 and Angora in 1925. Before 1400, and for states with no capital in the data, a capital is drawn as a city.

#### The sources and how they are combined

`tools/build-cities.mjs` combines these, in this order:

1. **Reba, Reitsma, and Seto (2016):** Tertius Chandler's (1987) and George Modelski's (2003) estimates of city populations, from 3700 BC to 1975, geocoded: 1,597 cities from Chandler and 154 from Modelski. For dates up to AD 1000, Modelski's figures are used, and Chandler's only where Modelski has none within 60 years; after 1000, Chandler's. A figure that is four times higher or lower than both its neighbours (within 400 years) is dropped as a misprint, such as Delhi's 1,250,000 in 1375 or Baghdad's 10,000 in 1150. Modelski's list for 2000 is not used: several of its cities are geocoded to the wrong place.
2. **UCHRONIA's 3,060 cities** (GeoNames), the cities of today. A Reba city joins one of them when it has the same name, or one of its other names, within 60 km, or lies within 8 km and has figures from 1850 on (so ancient Merv does not become modern Bayramaly).
3. **Natural Earth's populated places**, matched by name within 40 km, give each city its Wikidata id, today's population (`POP_MIN`, the city proper), and, for about 590 cities, the UN's World Urbanization Prospects figures from 1950 to 2020.
4. **Hanson (2016):** J. W. Hanson's Cities Database of 1,388 cities of the Greek and Roman world, with their founding dates, as published by the SDAM project with population estimates from their areas. A city joins an existing one within 8 km with the same modern name, or within 3 km. Its figure counts for AD 100, and is held from its founding date.
5. **historical-basemaps' dated places:** the 70 of the 2,340 places in its `places.geojson` that have a founding year (the undated ones are left out, because showing them would put ruins on maps of centuries when they were not lived in). `PLACE_DROP` removes four that are not settlements (Calusa, Levisa, Cumbe Mayo, and the Ōyu stone circles), and `PLACE_EDIT` adds end years to places long abandoned (Ur, Uruk, Harappa, Great Zimbabwe, Taxila, and others), corrects the dates of Xanadu and Vijayanagara, and gives some places names by year. Both tables are in `tools/city-tables.mjs`.
6. **`CITY_ADD`:** a few cities the sources lack, such as Karakorum, Nineveh, Persepolis, Mapungubwe, and Koumbi Saleh, with their dates.
7. **United States census, 1790 to 2010:** Erik Steiner's *Historical U.S. City Populations* (Stanford CESTA), `1790-2010_MASTER.csv`, the census population of 8,915 places by decade. A city of the table joins it by name within 20 km and takes its census figures in place of the other sources' for those years (up to 1940 where the UN's figures from 1950 exist); a place that ranked among the hundred largest of the country at some census but is not a city of today, and lies more than 3 km from one, is added (`US_TOP`): Brooklyn until it joined New York in 1898, and Roxbury, Dorchester, Kensington, and Williamsburgh until their annexations. Such a place ends ten years after its last census figure of 2,500 or more (`US_GONE`), if that came before 1990.
8. **Wikidata, for cities with no figure before 1900:** `tools/cities-wikidata.txt`, one line per city, `Q-id|founding year|year:population,…`, from each city's inception (P571, the earliest) and its populations (P1082) dated before 1960. It was fetched once, through a browser on Noah's computer, for the 1,498 cities of the table whose figures all start in 1890 or later; 836 had something. The populations are taken as figures before the city's first one; the founding year only as the earliest year the city can appear, and only when it comes before the city's first figure, since many inceptions are later administrative dates.

`CITY_POP` adds population points where a city rose or fell faster than the sources' figures, decades apart, can show, so that the page does not interpolate smoothly across a sack or a plague: the Mongol sacks of Samarkand, Bukhara, Merv, Nishapur, Gurganj, Kiev, Vladimir, Zhongdu, and Baghdad; Acre in 1291 and Antioch in 1268; Timur's sacks of Delhi, Sarai, and Baghdad; and the Black Death in Florence, Siena, Venice, Paris, and Cairo. Each entry gives a range of years in which the source's own figures are dropped, and the points that replace them. The thinning of the city figures keeps these points even when they are less than 20 years apart.

`REBA_FIX` corrects cities the source geocoded wrongly (and moves Mayapan off Mérida, and parts Old Oyo from modern Oyo in 1836): Kalyani (Chandler's "Kalyan" before 1500, placed at Kalyan near Mumbai), Shangqiu (placed at Shanghai), Cahokia and St. Louis, Sarai, Djenné, Niani, Memphis (Egypt before 1000, Tennessee after), Richmond (Virginia) and Hamilton (Ontario), which the source gave with eastern longitudes, and others. A Reba city with figures from 1850 that matches nothing near it, and has a namesake among today's cities within 1,000 km, is moved to it (Minneapolis, Ohrid, and three others; the build lists them).

#### Dates and gaps

- **First year:** the founding year in `CITY_SINCE` if there is one (Baghdad 762, Edo 1457, St. Petersburg 1703, New York 1624, Lelystad 1967, and about 240 others); otherwise the earliest of Hanson's founding date, historical-basemaps' date, and the first figure. A city whose first figure is earlier than 1850 and has no other date appears 100 years before it, at a quarter of that figure. A city of the United States census appears when its census figures, or their projection back along the world's urban growth, first reach 5,000 (`APPEAR`), and no earlier than ten years before its first census figure. Any other city whose figures start from 1850 appears when they, or their projection back, first reach 5,000, no earlier than 100 years before its first figure and not before its Wikidata founding year; one with no figure at all, only today's size, when that size projected back along the world's urban growth first reaches 5,000, and not before 1850 (`UNKNOWN_FROM`). Until 27 October 2026 every city of today with no figure before 1900 appeared in 1900, about 2,550 at once. A founding date with no figure for more than 100 years after it starts the city at a quarter of its first figure, but no more than 2,000 (`EARLY_CAP`).
- **Last year:** `CITY_UNTIL` (Cahokia 1350, Angkor 1431, Vijayanagara 1565, Teotihuacan 650, and others), or a date from Hanson or historical-basemaps. A city that is not a city of today ends 100 years after its last figure, falling to a quarter of it. A Roman town known only from Hanson ends when its region's ancient names give way (see below), at half its size.
- **Gaps:** Chandler lists only the largest cities of each date, so a city missing from his lists was smaller. Where two figures are more than 250 years apart, the city falls to a third of the smaller one for the years between, 100 years after the first and before the second.
- **The years from 1850:** for a city of today, a missing figure for 1950 is estimated from the next figure scaled by the growth of the world's urban population (17% of 2020's in 1950, 5% in 1900, 2.7% in 1850), but not above 200,000, the smallest city in Chandler's 1950 list. A missing figure for 1900 is estimated the same way, but not above 40,000, about the smallest in his 1900 list. A city with no figure at all for today takes its UCHRONIA tier's typical size.

#### Names by date

Cities are named as an English-language atlas of the time would print them: the English name where English had one in common use (Rome, Athens, Constantinople, Moscow, Peking, Canton, Bombay), otherwise the name used locally at the time in its usual Latin-script form (Lutetia, Eboracum, Edo, Danzig, Pressburg, Léopoldville). For the Greek and Roman world this is the usage of the Barrington Atlas, through Hanson: Latin names in the West and Greek in the East, except where English has its own name (Athens, Carthage, Syracuse). A name changes on the date it was officially changed. Where the English form changed without a renaming, the change follows English usage:

- Chinese cities have pinyin names before 1800 (Chang'an, Lin'an, Khanbaliq, Jiankang), the postal and Wade–Giles forms of English atlases from 1800 (Peking, Nanking, Canton, Tientsin, Sian), and pinyin again from 1 January 1979, when English usage switched to it. The postal forms come from the alternative names in Chandler's data.
- Indian and Ukrainian cities change on the date the new form was made official (Bombay became Mumbai in 1995, and Lvov became Lviv in 1991).
- Turkish cities take their Turkish names on 28 March 1930, when the Turkish post office stopped accepting the old ones (Constantinople, Angora, Smyrna, Adrianople, Trebizond, Brusa, Konia).

`CITY_NAMES` in `tools/city-tables.mjs` holds the names by date of about 420 cities, keyed by the city's name today (`"Name|lon"`, with the longitude rounded to tens, tells apart cities of the same name). The first name in each list applies from the city's first year. Examples: Byzantium, Constantinople, Istanbul; Eboracum, Eoforwic, Jórvík, York; Lutetia, Paris; Edo, Tokyo; New Amsterdam, New York; Sunda Kelapa, Jayakarta, Batavia, Jakarta; Christiania, Oslo; Tsaritsyn, Stalingrad, Volgograd; Pressburg, Bratislava; Tenochtitlan, Mexico City; Yathrib, Medina; Heian-kyō, Kyoto; Thăng Long, Hanoi; Salisbury, Harare.

A city from Hanson's database that is also a city of today, and has no entry in the table, keeps its Latin or Greek name until its region's ancient names give way, by `REGION_SWITCH`: 500 in the Latin West, 640 in Syria, Palestine, Egypt, and Arabia (the Arab conquests), 698 in North Africa, 1300 in Anatolia, and 1400 in the Balkans and Greece (the Turkish conquests). These dates are approximate. About 2,000 more cities that no longer exist, or that the data shows only in the past, are shown under their names of the time (Babylon, Memphis, Taxila, Merv, Cahokia, Vijayanagara, and Hanson's towns).

- `roman_cities_pop.csv` from `data/` of [sdam-au/social_diversity](https://github.com/sdam-au/social_diversity);
- `ne_10m_populated_places.geojson` from `geojson/` of [nvkelso/natural-earth-vector](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector);
- `1790-2010_MASTER.csv` from `data/` of [cestastanford/historical-us-city-populations](https://github.com/cestastanford/historical-us-city-populations).

UCHRONIA's `cities.json` is read from `../../uchronia/data/cities.json` by default. The build takes about two seconds. It lists the table keys it can't match, the cities it moved, and the counts; `--check` also prints the 25 largest cities at a series of dates. It writes the cities that rank among the 500 largest at any decade into `data/cities-dated.json` (270 KB, 68 KB gzipped) and the rest into `data/cities-dated-minor.json` (230 KB, 94 KB gzipped).

A row is `[id, name, lon, lat, first year, last year or null, [year, population in thousands, …], names by date or 0, Wikidata id or 0]`. Years are decimal: 1924.07 is late January 1924, and a whole year means 1 January. Names by date are `[[year, name], …]`, the first from -9999. The id is the city's name today, in lower case with hyphens, made unique; it stays the same across builds while the sources do.

### First-level divisions

The internal first-level divisions of some countries, such as the states and territories of the United States, are drawn inside them from the dates their boundaries are known. They come from two sources:

- **The United States, from 3 September 1783:** the Newberry Library's [Atlas of Historical County Boundaries](https://publications.newberry.org/ahcb/) (AHCB), whose layer of states and territories dates every change of a state's or territory's boundary to the day, from the Treaty of Paris to 31 December 2000. CHRONIA uses its version generalised to 0.01°, as converted to GeoJSON in the [USAboundariesData](https://github.com/ropensci/USAboundariesData) package (`data-raw/historical/histstates.geojson`). It keeps the states, territories, unorganised territories, and the District of Columbia, and leaves out the AHCB's areas of type "Other", which were never divisions of the United States: the Vermont Republic, the Texas Republic, Oregon Country, the State of Deseret, and an area of uncertain boundary. No state's boundaries have changed since 2000, so the shapes in force on 31 December 2000 run on to today.
- **Other countries, from the date their present boundaries have held:** Natural Earth's admin-1 states and provinces, through UCHRONIA's `data/provinces.json` at the detail of its `provinces-detail.json`, whose arcs line up with it. Present-day shapes are used only from the date since which they have not changed, with older names, and with divisions merged or cut where they were created later, from the builder's table `NE_REGIONS`:
  - Canada from 15 May 1912 (the extensions of Quebec, Ontario, and Manitoba): Newfoundland from 31 March 1949, Nunavut, part of the Northwest Territories until then, from 1 April 1999, and the names Yukon Territory until 31 March 2003 and Newfoundland until 5 December 2001;
  - Australia from federation on 1 January 1901: the Australian Capital Territory part of New South Wales and the Northern Territory part of South Australia until 1911, North Australia and Central Australia, divided at 20° south, from 1 March 1927 to 11 June 1931, and the Federal Capital Territory under that name until 28 July 1938;
  - Mexico from 11 January 1935, when Quintana Roo was restored: the territories of Baja California Norte (a state from 16 January 1952), Baja California Sur, and Quintana Roo (states from 8 October 1974), and the Federal District (Mexico City from 29 January 2016);
  - West Germany from 25 April 1952 (Baden-Württemberg), with the Saarland from 1 January 1957, and the eastern states and Berlin from 3 October 1990;
  - Austria from 1 January 1958, after Vienna's boundaries were settled in 1954 and adjusted in 1956 and 1957;
  - China from 26 April 1988 (Hainan), with Chongqing part of Sichuan until 13 March 1997;
  - India from 30 May 1987 (Goa), with Chhattisgarh, Uttaranchal (Uttarakhand from 2007), and Jharkhand part of their old states until November 2000, Telangana part of Andhra Pradesh until 1 June 2014, and Ladakh part of Jammu and Kashmir until 30 October 2019; Orissa and Pondicherry under those names until 2011 and 2006;
  - Brazil from 15 March 1975, when Guanabara joined Rio de Janeiro: Mato Grosso do Sul part of Mato Grosso until 1 January 1979, Tocantins part of Goiás until the constitution of 5 October 1988, and the territories of Rondônia (a state from 4 January 1982), Roraima, and Amapá (states from 5 October 1988);
  - Argentina from 1 January 1956, after the national territories became provinces in 1951 to 1955 and the provinces named Presidente Perón and Eva Perón took back the names Chaco and La Pampa: the National Territory of Tierra del Fuego until Law 23,775 of 26 April 1990, and the Federal Capital until the city's constitution of 1 October 1996;
  - Spain from 25 February 1983, when the last statutes of autonomy came into force, with its 17 autonomous communities put together from Natural Earth's provinces (`ES_COMMUNITIES`), and Ceuta and Melilla from their statutes of 14 March 1995;
  - France from 9 January 1970, when Corsica became a region of its own: the 22 metropolitan regions, put together from Natural Earth's departments (`FR_OLD`), Centre as Centre-Val de Loire from 17 January 2015, and the 13 regions from 1 January 2016 (`FR_MERGED`), under their provisional names until the decrees of 28 September 2016 named Grand Est, Hauts-de-France, Nouvelle-Aquitaine, and Occitania; with Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, and Réunion, and Mayotte from 31 March 2011;
  - Japan from 1 April 1893, when the Tama districts passed from Kanagawa to Tokyo, with Tokyo Prefecture as Tokyo from 1 July 1943;
  - Nigeria from 1 October 1996, when the last six states were created.

  Italy is left out, because its regions have changed in small ways as late as 2017 (Valmarecchia in 2009, Sappada in 2017), and Natural Earth has only the present ones.

`tools/build-divisions.mjs` writes one file per country into `data/divisions/`, and the list of them into `data/divisions.json`. It takes the path to the npm modules, the AHCB GeoJSON, and optionally UCHRONIA's `provinces.json`, and runs in a few seconds, separately from the main builder:

```
git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/ropensci/USAboundariesData.git
node chronia/tools/build-divisions.mjs node_modules USAboundariesData/data-raw/historical/histstates.geojson
```

What it does:

1. Each division is a span of days with one shape, `[name, first day, last day, shape]`, where days count from 1 January 1886 as in `modern.json` (so days before 1886 are negative) and a last day of `null` means the span runs to the end. A renaming is a new span with the same shape.
2. All of a country's shapes go into one TopoJSON, `topo`, so the boundaries shared between spans are stored once. Only the internal lines are drawn, so only the arcs that two divisions in force at the same time share are kept in detail (simplified to about 0.5 km, `HI_KM`, and quantized to 200,000 steps); the other arcs, along coasts and the country's own border, are simplified to about 12 km, since they serve only to find a division under a click and to place its name.
3. Pieces that simplifying turns inside out (two tiny islands of North Carolina) are dropped, and where a division is cut from a Natural Earth shape (North and Central Australia, at 20° south), the cut points are added to its neighbours too, so that their shared boundaries stay shared arcs.
4. `labels` holds, for each shape, `[lon, lat, radius, half room, area, middle lon, middle radius, middle half room]`: the pole of inaccessibility of its largest piece and the radius of the largest circle inside it in km; the room along the parallel through the pole, to the nearer edge; its area in km²; and the same for the middle of that parallel's run across the piece, for long shapes whose pole sits off centre.
5. A row in `divisions.json` is `[key, names of the country on the map, first day, last day, [west, south, east, north], file]`.

The 14 files come to about 613 KB, or 219 KB compressed (us 166 KB, cn 84 KB, in 60 KB, ca 59 KB, br 55 KB, mx 36 KB, ng 27 KB, jp 26 KB, ar 22 KB, fr 22 KB, de 17 KB, es 17 KB, au 16 KB, at 6 KB), and none is loaded at the start. A file is loaded only when its country is on the screen.

### Rows in the snapshot files

A row in a map file is `[id, ruler id, part-of id, precision, people, label]`, with ids into `polities` in `eras.json`. A missing row value is `-1`, and unnamed land is `[-1]`. A `label` is `[lon, lat, radius km, room km, angle°, area km², spots]`.

A `polities` entry is `[name, colour, years on the snapshot maps, Wikipedia title or [[year, title], …], people, [first year, last year] from 1886 or null, [first year, last year] until 1885 or null]`, where people is 0 for a state, 1 for a people, and 2 for a band, with an optional eighth item, `[[first day, colour], …]`, for a colour that changes from a date (see `COLOUR_FROM` under [Building](#building)).

## Drawing

### The engine

The projection and tracing come from UCHRONIA's `app.js`: `prepare`, `traceRing`, `traceLine`, `traceFlat`, `featureInFront`, and their helpers. Shapes are turned once into unit vectors, and each frame projects them with the view's basis vectors.

- **On the globe:** points behind the horizon are pushed to the rim. A shape that might wrap round the far side, meaning its bounding cap holds the point opposite the view, is drawn through d3 instead.
- **On the flat map:** shapes are unwrapped in longitude and drawn up to three times, so the map scrolls without end once it is wider than the window.
  - When the whole map fits in the window, the copies either side would show past its frame, so everything from the land to the borders is clipped to the frame (`traceOutline`), as UCHRONIA does. The sea is already clipped the same way.

CHRONIA first used d3's own path with clipping for everything. It took about 190 ms a frame in headless Chromium, against about 35 ms now.

### The order of a frame

1. **The sea:** ocean, deep water, the graticule, and the ripple lines along the coast.
   - UCHRONIA's deep water comes in tiles whose edges are long straight runs along meridians and parallels, which the globe would draw as chords, leaving a sliver of shallow water along 180°. `densify` adds a point every degree along them (`SEA_STEP`) before they are prepared.
   - These are kept in an offscreen canvas and redrawn only when the view or the dated land changes. Scrubbing through the years therefore seldom redraws them.
   - While the map moves, one ripple is drawn instead of up to four.
   - The ripples scale with the island: an island gets one ripple for every 9 px of its size, up to the usual four, and islands under 9 px get none.
2. **The land:**
   - Clipped to Natural Earth land, as it was at the date (see [Geography over time](#geography-over-time)), the land is filled with paper colour, then with each state's colour, peoples' colour, and unnamed land.
   - Islands smaller than about 1.6 px across at the current zoom are left out of the land, the coastline, and the clip, so the open sea isn't scattered with specks.
   - Close in, from a scale of 5,000 px per radian (`FINE_S`, about 0.8 px a kilometre), the land, the coastline, and the clip come from `data/land-detail.json` (1.9 MB), about three and a half times as many points along the coast as `land.json`'s `hi` level, the detail UCHRONIA uses close in. `landKey` fetches it the first time it is needed and uses `hi` until it has arrived, or where a change to the land is in force that the geography has no patch for at this level.
   - The historical shapes' coastlines don't match Natural Earth's exactly. So before the fills, each state's coast is stroked about 40 km wide in its colour inside the land clip. That fills the slivers between the two coastlines, and the fills then cover it everywhere else. Only the parts of a state's outline that run near a coast are stroked this way, so a colour never spills over an inland edge. Hovering over a state, or selecting it, tints its shape and the same coastal stroke, clipped to the land outside every shape (`coastOf` finds the state's own coastal arcs), so the tint covers the same land as its colour, such as the strip of the North Sea Canal that CShapes leaves out of the Netherlands.
3. **Relief:** UCHRONIA's relief image is laid over the land through `sat.js`, at 72% "overlay". Closer in, as in UCHRONIA, the view's own terrain tiles take over once the map has stopped moving: `maybeRelief` works out the zoom level (6 to 11) and the tiles that cover the window (40 at most), fetches the Terrarium elevation tiles from AWS (`s3.amazonaws.com`, allowed in the page's `img-src`), shades each into a hillshade (`hillshade`, the same lights and exaggeration as UCHRONIA's), and hands them to `sat.js`, which draws them in place of the world image where they have arrived. If the tiles cannot be fetched, the world image stays.
4. **Colour bands:** along every border and coast, a band is drawn inside each state, as in UCHRONIA's Antique style.
   - Along a border between two colours, the band follows the border and is clipped to the state's own shape.
   - Along a coast, the band follows Natural Earth's coastline, not the state's own coast, so it sits on the coast at every zoom and never doubles it. Each stretch of coast takes the band colour of the state that covers it. This is drawn into a separate layer: the states are filled in their band colours, and the result is kept only within a band along the coast. Islands too small for a band are tinted whole in their band colour instead.
   - The layer is redrawn only when the view, the set of shapes, or the dated land changes, at the screen's resolution, or half of it on high-density screens, and lower while the map moves. Hovering and redraws that change none of these reuse it.
5. **Hover and selection:** a hovered state is tinted. A selected state is tinted red and outlined in red.
6. **Water:** the marshes of the date (see [Marshes](#marshes)), then the rivers, then the lakes of the date over them, so a river that runs through a lake or reservoir stops at its shore and starts again past it, whichever lake set it comes from. Lakes appear from about 6 px across. Those under about 18 px have a faint water-coloured shore instead of an inked one, so a land of small lakes, like Canada or Finland, doesn't become a scatter of outlines. Rivers start at a scale of about 520 px for the globe's radius.
7. **Lines:** the first-level divisions (see [First-level divisions](#first-level-divisions-1)), then the coastline, then the borders, each clipped to land:
   - between a dependency and its ruler, or two dependencies of one ruler: thin and faint;
   - between two members of the Holy Roman Empire, or two Roman provinces: a fine broken line in border ink (see [The Holy Roman Empire](#the-holy-roman-empire));
   - between other states: solid;
   - soft borders: dotted. A border is soft only when either side has precision 1 or is a people, in every year; until 27 October 2026 every border on the snapshot maps before 1200 was soft, and Cliopatria's states before 1200 now have firm borders like its later ones. Until 27 October 2026 every border before 1648 was dotted, which hid the difference between a firm border and a guessed one.
   - a band has no border: an edge between a band and a band, or a band and blank land, is not drawn, and an edge between a band and a state is drawn in the state's style.
   - Where a state's edge meets no other shape on land, as in the interior of Arabia or the central Sahara around 1890, the edge is drawn as a border too, in that state's style, with its band.
   - Lines get slightly heavier as the map zooms in, from a scale of about 700 px to 4,000 px: solid borders from 1.15 to 1.7 px, the coastline from 0.75 to 1.1 px, and the dots of soft borders from 1.6 to 2.1 px.
8. **Finishing:** the aged paper (seams, vignette, and highlight on the globe; darkened top and bottom on the flat map), and the double rim. The seams run along every 30° of longitude, so they are drawn only when the lines of latitude and longitude are on.
9. **Labels:**
   - **States and peoples** first, largest first, but names shown in the last frame go before names that were not, so a name that is showing keeps its place against a newcomer (see [Names of states and peoples](#names-of-states-and-peoples)).
   - **Seas** (the same list and scales as UCHRONIA).
   - **Capitals and cities** (see [Cities](#cities)). They come after the names of states and keep clear of them: a capital's mark gives way to a state's name, not the reverse. A name goes to the right of its mark, or else to the left, above, or below, wherever it doesn't collide.
   - **First-level divisions**, last, so they never cover the others.

   Labels never overlap one another or the page's panels.

The `hi` level is used when the globe's radius is at least 900 px and the map is still, or at 2,600 px and over. Otherwise `lo` is used.

### Names of states and peoples

A state's name is laid out once per map on the ground, in longitude and latitude, and the screen only projects it, scales it, and decides whether it shows. So panning and turning the globe move a name with the land, zooming changes its size smoothly, and the names don't reshuffle when the view settles.

- **On the ground:** for each state, the page takes its shape at the `lo` level and maps it into a plane around a starting spot: on the globe a sinusoidal projection in a frame whose parallels are the parallels of latitude (tilted towards the spot when it lies beyond 60°), on the flat map the map's own longitude and latitude. In that plane a name is a straight box, so its place is found as the pole of inaccessibility for a box of the name's own proportions, by a cell search like polylabel's that measures room as the largest box that fits rather than the largest circle. The search is done for each form of the name: one, two, or three lines (a hyphenated name like Congo-Léopoldville can break after the hyphen), and, when the shape is clearly long (its axes differ by more than 1.5 times), turned along its main axis, but never more than 30° (35° for names of six letters or fewer). Each form also gets a version with tighter letter spacing.
- **Near the middle:** as in UCHRONIA, the pole gives only the largest name that fits, not its place. A second cell search, ordered by distance from the centroid of the state's largest piece, finds the spot nearest that centroid where a name at least 78% of the pole's size still fits (`CENTRE_KEEP`), and the name is set there, sized to the room it has. So a name sits in the middle of its country rather than wherever the most room happens to be, and gives up at most a fifth of its size for it. The pole is kept as a fallback form, counted 25% smaller, for when the name near the middle is too small to show or collides. For the further spots of a large state (below), the spot itself stands in for the centroid. On the globe, if the name near the middle is more than about 3.4° from the starting spot, the frame is moved there and the search repeated, so the frame is centred on the name.
- **Spots:** the starting spots are the builder's pole and its extra spots for large states. For a state over about 7° across, a grid of further spots, about a fifth of the state's size apart, is added when needed: when none of its names can be shown, the spots that lie on the screen are worked out, two or three at a time, so that a name stays on screen when the map is zoomed into part of a large state. Once the map stops moving, the first spot, near the middle, is always tried before such a further spot, even one already showing, so a name moved to the side of a large state while zoomed in goes back to the middle when it can, rather than staying small against a border.
- **Capitals:** both searches treat the state's capital as a small obstacle, so the name sits beside its capital rather than on it, unless keeping clear of the capital costs the name more than 15% of its size, as in a small state whose capital is in its middle.
- **On the screen:** each form's size is the room it has at its spot near the middle, at 88%, scaled by the zoom. It is capped by a size for the state's area on the screen, 5 + 3.6 × log₂(side ÷ 10 px), where the side is the square root of the area, between 7.5 px and 26 px (peoples 85% of that, up to 15 px), so names grow with their countries. Names are in capitals, peoples' in italics. The form with the largest size at its pole wins, with tilted forms, extra lines, and tight spacing counting 20%, 10 to 18%, and 10% less. The form and spot shown last are tried first, and kept while they fit and no other form would be more than 25% larger, so a name changes form only at clear zoom thresholds and never because the map is panned.
- **Showing:** a name shows at 7.5 px or more (peoples 8.5 px), and once shown keeps showing down to 7% less. A newly shown name needs some free room around it (2 px and 15% of its size), so it isn't pushed off again by a neighbour as the map zooms. A name whose baseline runs more than about 30° from the horizontal on the screen (35° for short names), as happens along the parallels near the edge of the globe, is not shown; a name already showing is allowed about 14° more before it gives way. Names fade in and out over 150 ms, or appear at once with reduced motion.
- **At the edges:** a newly shown name must fit wholly on the screen, clear of the header, the year bar, the key, and the card. Once shown, a name keeps its spot, form, and size while any letter of it is still on screen and not under those panels: it may run off the edge of the screen or under a panel, and is only placed again elsewhere, or dropped, when it has gone from sight altogether. It still keeps clear of other names and of the globe's rim. An offshore name (below) is kept the same way, on the side it was on, while its state is partly on screen. The panels are marked `ui` in `fixedBoxes()`; `insideMap()` tests the globe's disc or the flat map's band, `insideView()` adds the screen's edges, and `inSight()` asks whether any letter is still visible.
- **A slight overlap:** a large state (over 64 px across), or any state over 28 px on a map zoomed out below a scale of 600 px, whose name doesn't quite fit at 7.5 px still gets it at 7.5 px if it has at least 85% of the room it needs, so it may cross its border a little.
- **Offshore:** a state under 64 px across, fully in view (or partly, once its name is showing), with the globe's radius at least 600 px, whose name doesn't fit inside gets a 9 px name in the sea against its coast: beside its middle for a tall state, below or above it for a wide one, or at a corner, wherever the box is clear of land. The side used last is kept.
- **Curved names:** on the globe each name follows its line on the sphere, so its baseline bends with the globe, like the names on a printed globe. Each letter is drawn with the projection's own scale and turn at its place, so letters near the edge narrow with the globe's curve. Each letter stands square to its own baseline (its height keeps the projection's scale but not the meridian's slant), so a name on a curve fans out like lettering on an arc, wider on the outside of the bend. A line that is straight to within 0.3 px on the screen is drawn in one piece. The flat map's names are straight. Collisions are tested on the boxes of runs of three letters (one box per line when it is straight), not on one box for the name, so a slanted or curved name doesn't block a whole neighbouring country.
- **Level names on a turned globe:** when the globe is turned so that north isn't up where a country is, a name laid along its parallel looks slanted on the screen, and past the tilt limit it can't be used. So the names are also laid at the angle on the ground that reads level on the screen, and centred like the others, so the name stays in the middle of its land rather than moving to a corner where some other slant fits (Iran's used to move to its north-west or south-east). These forms are marked down a little, so a large name that follows the globe still wins when it fits, and while the globe turns the name keeps its angle until it is about 9° off.

**Keeping it fast:** the layout on the ground is kept for each state, keyed by its name, its shape's label numbers, and its capital, so a state whose shape doesn't change between two maps, as with most states from one month to the next, isn't laid out again. Laying out a whole new map takes about 90 to 150 ms in headless Chromium (the maps of 1815, 1965, and 1975; about twice as long as before the search near the middle was added), and placing the names for a view about 1 to 11 ms; the placement is kept for each map and view, so redrawing for hovering or the card costs nothing. While the map moves or the year is dragged, new layouts are worked out for at most 16 ms a frame, and a state without one uses its layout from the last map that had it until the view settles. `__chronia.timeLabels(n)` times the placement (`polities`), the cities (`places`), and a full layout on the ground (`ground`).

### Disputed land

A span with claimants (status 6) is drawn in stripes rather than one colour: diagonal bands, 6 px wide and turned 45°, one in each claimant's colour on the day the span starts (`stripes(cols)`, cached by colour). The stripes are fixed to the ground rather than the screen: each frame, `anchorStripes()` moves every stripe pattern with a point of the map near the centre of the view, so panning, turning the globe, and zooming carry the stripes with the land. When that point goes off the map, or more than 0.6 of the globe's radius from its centre, a new point near the centre takes over with the same offset, so the stripes never jump. Its edges are dotted, like an approximate border, and the claimants' colour bands run along them on their side. Hovering it reads *Jammu and Kashmir, disputed between India and Pakistan, held by India*, the card begins *Disputed between India and Pakistan on …*, with each claimant a link, and the key shows a row for it when the map has any. In `level()` the disputed shapes go into `L.disputes`, one group per set of claimant colours, and are filled after the states.

### Shapes over each other

The sources overlap: Cliopatria's polities with each other and with historical-basemaps' fills, peoples with states, and a state's spans from different sources with each other. Before 1886 the builder had already kept the smaller shape where two of Cliopatria's overlapped; `resolveOverlaps` now takes every pair of spans that overlap by 50 km² or more at the same time, at the end of the build for 1200 to 1885 and for 1886 on, and decides by these rules, in order:

1. **The same state:** the smaller span gives up the overlap.
2. **A people and a state:** the people gives way; of two peoples, the smaller.
3. **A ruler and its dependency:** the dependency keeps the land; of two dependencies of one ruler, the smaller.
4. **A state inside another:** an overlap of 80% or more of the smaller state (`NEST_SHARE`) is left to the smaller, as a vassal or a nation the sources draw inside a larger empire's claim: the Regency of Algiers and the Crimean Khanate inside the Ottoman Empire, Bijapur and Golconda inside the Mughal Empire of 1675, the nations of the West inside the Spanish Empire's and Rupert's Land's claims.
5. **An approximate border** (precision 1) gives way to a firmer one; of two, the larger gives way. Most of the overlapping homelands of North America's nations before 1500, from historical-basemaps, fall under this rule, so they are cut rather than shown as disputes.
6. **Two firmly drawn states** overlapping by 10,000 km² or more, and by a tenth of the smaller (`DISPUTE_KM2` and `DISPUTE_SHARE`), give the overlap to a new span of *Disputed land* between them, with no name drawn (see [Disputed land](#disputed-land)).
7. **Anything else** is cut from the larger, so the smaller keeps it, as `MAPMAKING.md` asks.

The cuts are made only for the years the two overlap, with `reshape`. The build prints how many overlaps each rule settled. A pair is tested when the smaller's 0.25° cells meet the larger's, or, for a smaller shape of up to 40 cells (`NEAR_CELLS`), lie next to them, so two small shapes that overlap without sharing a cell centre are no longer missed; and when polygon-clipping returns an empty intersection for a pair that shares more than two cells, the intersection is tried again with the points rounded to a millionth of a degree (the build reports these as `retried`), since polygon-clipping sometimes answers wrongly without throwing.

### Meeting the lines of 1886

Cliopatria draws its borders on a coarse grid, in steps 7 to 20 km wide, and CShapes, used from 1886, follows the surveyed lines, so every border used to change its look between 1885 and 1886. After the overlaps of 1200 to 1885 are settled, `snapTo1886` gives the earlier spans CShapes' lines of 1 January 1886 wherever the two differ only by those steps:

1. **The pieces of 1886:** each piece of a state in CShapes on that day, simplified to 0.3 km (`SNAP_KM`), with its points rounded to a millionth of a degree.
2. **Whose piece:** a span of a state, not a people, a small territory, or disputed land, that lasts into 1500 or later (`SNAP_FROM`) takes up a piece when at least half the piece's 0.25° cells lie in the span (`SNAP_OWN`).
3. **The steps:** the part of the piece outside the span falls into pieces. Those under 800 km² (`SNAP_SMALL_KM2`), or under 15 km across, measured as twice the area over the perimeter (`SNAP_THIN_KM`), are the grid's steps, if they touch the span. Larger ones are a real difference of border, and the span keeps its own line there, as it does with pieces that don't touch it, such as an island or a colony that CShapes counts as part of the state. Small territories and disputed land of the same years keep what they hold.
4. **The neighbours:** the span takes only the parts of the steps that another span of those years held, and that span gives them up, only for the years the two overlap. A neighbour that would give up more than a fifth of its land at once (`SNAP_SPARE`) keeps it, so a small state inside the 1886 shape, such as the Mughal Empire of 1857, is not taken. Steps in the sea or in blank land are not taken, so the coasts keep Cliopatria's line, which the page's coast covers in any case, and the files grow less. Spans of one state in a row that take the same steps are dealt with together, and a neighbour's cut that is the same in consecutive spans is made once.
5. **The new shape:** the span is joined with the piece, and the larger differences and the steps it doesn't take are cut out again, so the steps it takes become part of its land rather than pieces beside it. To keep the step quick, a shape is first cut to the box of the piece it is measured against.
6. **Hairlines:** pieces of a changed span under 1 km² and under 50 m across (`SNAP_HAIR_KM2` and `SNAP_HAIR_KM`), left by the rounding, are dropped.

A border that did not move before 1886 therefore has CShapes' line back to the last time it changed, and one that did move keeps Cliopatria's line in the years before. The step takes about 9 minutes of the build, and makes the files from 1500 to 1885 about two-thirds larger (1800 to 1885 from 1.8 to 3.4 MB compressed), since the lines of 1886 have more points than Cliopatria's steps. The build prints how many spans of how many states took steps, the average area a span took, the neighbours' cuts, the neighbours spared, and the hairlines dropped.

### Pieces over each other

A span's pieces never overlap: when the builder adds pieces to a span (`reshape`), it joins them with `unionSafe`, which tries the whole join, then each piece on its own, then each with its points rounded to a millionth of a degree, and at the last adds only the part of a piece outside the span, so that a failed join no longer leaves pieces stacked on the span's own land. Before each topology is made, `dissolveFeat` checks every span of 1200 to 1885 and from 1886 for pieces over each other and joins them. Pieces stacked like this were drawn with a border line along each piece's edge inside the state, as in Arabia from 1886 to 1918 and Mandatory Palestine from 1920 to 1948. The page guards against what is left: `outerEdges` treats an arc that one shape uses twice (two of its own pieces meeting, or a slit) as inside the shape, not as an outer edge, and leaves out arcs of zero length, which the quantizing makes where two pieces meet at a point. Both were drawn as a broken border with its band, or as dots, inside the Ottoman Empire's Iraq from 1886 to 1918.

### Coasts and inland edges

To tell a state's coast from an inland edge, the page draws Natural Earth's land into a hidden equirectangular grid of 2,880 by 1,440 cells (0.125°), with a band about 32 km either side of every coastline cut out of it. A point is inland when its cell, and enough neighbouring cells to cover the same distance east and west at its latitude, are still land. Each state's outer edges, the ones it shares with no other shape on the map, are then split into coastal runs (stroked for the coast fill) and inland runs (drawn as borders). Once UCHRONIA's lakes have loaded, every lake of 800 km² or more (`BIG_LAKE_KM2`) that is shown at the date (a reservoir only from its year; see [Lakes](#lakes)) is cut out of the grid with the same band, as the dated lakes are, so a state's edge along a large lake, such as the Netherlands' along the IJsselmeer, is a shore rather than a border. The grid is first drawn from the coarse land (`lo`), so the first frame is quick, and an inland run is then extended up to about 45 km into the coastal stretch at each end, so that it reaches the shore.

A moment after the first map is shown, the grid is drawn again from the `hi` land, the land the map draws below a scale of 5,000 px (about a third of a second in headless Chromium), and once the finer coast has loaded (from a scale of 5,000 px), again from it, which takes about a second and a half (`gridBase` holds which land the grid was drawn from: `lo`, `hi`, or `fine`). Their rivers and estuaries are water that the coarse land fills in, so the edge of a state along an estuary, such as the United States along the Delaware below Philadelphia, is a shore rather than a border, and the coast fill covers the slivers of land between the state's edge and the finer river. Until 28 October 2026 the grid stayed coarse below a scale of 5,000 px, so a state's edge along an estuary that the coarse land fills in, such as West Germany's along the Elbe below Hamburg, was drawn as a thick border beside the river. With the `hi` and finer grids an inland run is extended only about 4 km, so that an inland stretch at the head of an estuary (the Delaware at Trenton) doesn't turn the whole estuary back into a border. The grid follows the dated land and the past lakes (see [Land](#land)).

The historical shapes' coasts also stop short of Natural Earth's in places, so a coastal city such as Naples or Edinburgh can be on land but in no shape. `polityAt`, which finds the state under the pointer for hovering, clicking, and `__chronia.hitAt`, then falls back to `polityNear`: it tries eight points round the spot, 0.06° away, then 0.12° and 0.18°, and takes the state that most of the first ring with any hits fall in.

### First-level divisions

- **When they load:** from a scale of 620 px for the globe's radius, with the layer on, the page loads `data/divisions.json`, then the file of each country whose dates hold the day shown and whose bounds are on the screen. Before 1886, the day is 1 July of the year shown, so the divisions follow the year even between two snapshot maps.
- **Lines:** for the day, the spans in force are found, and the arcs that two of them share are drawn. Each arc is converted once, and the last 64 sets of arcs are kept per country, so scrubbing through the years costs little. The lines are drawn after the rivers and before the coastline and the borders, clipped to the land and to the shapes of the country on the current map (`names` in `divisions.json`), so a division's line never runs into a neighbour, and parts of a division that the map gives to another state are not drawn. They are a fine broken line in brown ink, `--ch-division`, 0.65 to 1 px wide as the map zooms in, in dashes of about 4.2 widths and gaps of about 2.6.
- **Names:** from a scale of 1,250 px, after the names of states, capitals, and cities, so they never cover those. A name is in spaced capitals, 8 to 11 px by the division's size, in `--ch-division-label` with a thin parchment halo, on one line or two, at the division's pole or else at the middle of the parallel through it. It is placed only where it fits the room measured there, where the pole lies inside the country on the map, and where it is clear of every other label. The placement is kept until the view, the day, or the labels before it change.
- **The card:** when a state is clicked, the card also names the division under the click, such as "The place clicked is in Dakota Territory.", and follows it as the year changes. Under it, "Write about Dakota Territory in 1875" asks the note's server function for a note on the division in that period (`divNote`, kind `division`, with the division's name, the state's, and the year), shown in place; as for a state, a note already written appears by itself after a short peek. It is not shown when a state is picked from the card or by name.

## Geography over time

The land, lakes, and rivers are today's, from Natural Earth, at every date unless `data/geography.json` says otherwise. It holds the changes that are large enough to see at the map's scales and well documented. They follow the date shown, not the map: in 1150 the Yellow River runs south to the Yellow Sea, although the borders are still those of the map of 1100.

### Land

- **The Zuiderzee:** until 31 August 1930 the Zuiderzee is open sea: the IJsselmeer, the Markermeer, and the four polders. From then the Wieringermeer is land, and from 28 May 1932, when the Afsluitdijk closed it, the water is a lake, the IJsselmeer (see [Lakes](#lakes)).
- **The Fens:** the fen basin round the Wash, from Cambridge to Lincoln, is water joined to the Wash, part of the coastline, until it was drained: all of it until 1653, when the Bedford Level Corporation declared the Great Level drained after Vermuyden's works, and the Lincolnshire fens north of the River Welland (Deeping, Holland, and the East, West, and Wildmore Fens) until 1818, when the last of them was drained. The divide follows the Welland from Stamford by Market Deeping, Crowland, and Spalding to its outfall. The shore is the outline traced from elevation (see [Marshes](#marshes)), closed by 1.5 km and opened by 1 km so it runs smooth. Its islands of firmer ground are the outline's own holes of 4 km² or more (the Isle of Ely, and the silt round Boston and Spalding), and the fen islands that the shrunken peat no longer shows in the elevation, as ovals of a few kilometres round March, Whittlesey, Chatteris, Ramsey, and Thorney (`FEN_ISLANDS`). Rivers are drawn only on the land of the date, so none runs across the Fens' water, or the Zuiderzee's.
- **Caofeidian:** the reclaimed island in the Bohai Sea is left out before 2003, when the work began.
- **The Aral Sea:** Natural Earth's land has today's two parts of the Aral Sea as holes. Until August 2014 they are filled, so that the larger lakes of those years can be drawn over them.

A change to the land is stored as a patch of one polygon of `land.json` or `land-detail.json`, for each level (`hi`, `lo`, and `fine`), with the dates it holds between:

- `s`: a run of points of the outer ring and the points that replace it;
- `h`: holes to add, and `x`: holes to remove;
- `a`: polygons to add, such as Wieringen, Urk, and Marken while they were islands;
- `d`: the polygon is dropped.

The builder makes the patch by cutting the area of the old sea out of the land inside a box, so only the stretch of coast in the box changes. `datedLand(key)` applies the patches in force and keeps the last eight lists. The result takes the place of `land[key]` everywhere: the land clip, the coastline, the ripples, the coast band, and the sea layer. The sea layer's and the band's keys include the patches in force, so they are redrawn when the land changes.

`inland()` sees the dated land as well. When the patches or the past lakes in force change, `syncGrid()` redraws the coast grid only in the boxes that changed, from the polygons near them. A past lake counts as water there, with a band along its shore like a coast, so that a state's edge that ends on its shore, or under it, isn't drawn as a border across the water. Each map's borders are kept for each state of the grid, so a change of the land or of a past lake recomputes them once.

### Lakes

- **Reservoirs:** the 272 of UCHRONIA's lakes that Natural Earth marks as reservoirs, and ten more that are reservoirs though marked as lakes (such as Qadisiyah, Koka, and Dale Hollow). 147 appear from 1 January of the year Natural Earth gives for the dam, so Lake Nasser from 1970, Lake Volta from 1965, Lake Kariba from 1959, the Kuybyshev (Samara) reservoir from 1955, and Lake Mead from 1936. 85 that have no year in Natural Earth, or one that belongs to a later dam (Wheeler Lake, Trinity Lake, American Falls, Wyangala, and Orellana), take the year the dam was completed, listed in `YEAR_ADD` and `YEAR_FIX`: Guri 1968, Vilyuy 1967, Robert-Bourassa 1979, Tharthar 1956, and so on. 38 are natural lakes that a dam raised, such as Zaysan, Lac Saint-Jean, Oulujärvi, and the Arrow Lakes, or whose state before the dam is unclear, and are always shown (`RAISED`), as are 12 with no year found. Before each reservoir's year, Natural Earth's line of the river is drawn through it, so the Nile, the Volga, and the Zambezi run unbroken.
- **The Kakhovka Reservoir** is left out from 6 June 2023, when the dam was destroyed.
- **The Aral Sea:** the late-1950s extent of Natural Earth's historic lakes (67,500 km²) until 1987, when the sea split in two; then the single Aral Sea of Natural Earth 4.1 (18,400 km², about its extent in 2003 and 2004 by area) until August 2014, when the eastern basin dried; then today's North Aral Sea, South Aral Sea, and Barsakelmes Lake.
- **Lake Chad:** the early-1960s extent of Natural Earth's historic lakes (23,400 km²) until 1974, then today's.
- **Lop Nur:** Natural Earth's historic lake from 1921, when the Tarim's terminal lake moved back to Lop Nur, until 1964, when it had dried.
- **Lake Texcoco:** Natural Earth's historic lakes of the Valley of Mexico (early 1500s) until 1900, when the Gran Canal del Desagüe opened, then today's remnant.
- **New lakes:** the Salton Sea from 1905, Sarygamysh from 1960, Aydar Lake from February 1969, and the Great Bitter Lake from 18 March 1869, when it was filled from the Suez Canal.
- **The IJsselmeer:** from 28 May 1932 to 9 September 1942 it covers the Noordoostpolder and both Flevoland polders; to 29 June 1957, both Flevolands; to 29 May 1968, Southern Flevoland; then Natural Earth's lake of today.
- **The Haarlemmermeer:** a lake from 1647, when it had grown to about 150 km², until 1 July 1852, when it was dry.
- **Whittlesey Mere:** the largest of the Fenland meres, from 1653, when the Great Level round it was drained (before then it is part of the Fens' water), until it was drained in 1851. Its shape is not recorded closely enough to trace, so it is an ellipse of 3.8 by 2.6 km round 52.49° N, 0.225° W, about 7.8 km², near its summer extent of about 760 ha.

Past lakes lose their islands and holes under 0.5 km² (`simplePolys`), which the cuts along the polders' dikes left in the IJsselmeer as dots along its eastern and southern shores.

`lakesAt()` gives the lakes for the date, sorted by area, and is recomputed only when the date crosses one of the dates in the file. Rivers are clipped out of the past lakes, so the Amu Darya and the Syr Darya end at the shore of the year.

### Marshes

Large wetlands that were drained in a well-dated campaign are drawn until their draining, over the land and under the rivers: a wash of the sea colour at 40% and a pattern of small reed marks (`marshPattern`, 18 by 12 px) in the river colour, clipped to the land. They are shown once they are 8 px across or more (`MARSH_PX`).

- **The Pontine Marshes:** the plain between Cisterna, Terracina, and the coast, until 1935, when the reclamation begun in 1930 had drained most of it and the new towns of Littoria, Sabaudia, and Pontinia stood on it.

The Fens, which were drawn this way until 28 October 2026, are now part of the coastline (see [Land](#land)).

The outlines are traced from elevation, since no source maps the old wetlands' edges: the Terrarium elevation tiles at zoom 11 were contoured at 5 m for the Fens (about 3,760 km² on land, near the 3,900 km² usually given for Fenland) and at 25 m for the Pontine plain, the first inland from its coastal dunes (about 530 km² with the coastal lakes, against 750 to 800 km² in most accounts), smoothed and simplified to about 300 m. They are kept in `tools/wetlands.json`, so a rebuild needs no network. The land today is lower than the fens were, since drained peat shrinks, so the outline is the extent of the basin rather than of the water at any one time.

In `geography.json`, `marshes` is `[name, km², from, to, MultiPolygon]`.

### Rivers and canals

- **The Yellow River:** its lower course to the Bohai Sea, from the bend near Lankao, is drawn before 1128 and from 1 August 1855, when the river broke its north bank at Tongwaxiang. From 1128 to 1855 it is replaced by the southern course to the Yellow Sea. That course is an approximation: Natural Earth has no line for it, so it is drawn straight from the bend through the towns it passed (Shangqiu, Dangshan, Xuzhou, Suqian, Siyang, Huai'an, Lianshui, and Binhai) to the coast. Before the 16th century the river wandered among several channels to the Huai, and before 1128 its courses north of Shandong were not today's, so both are shown only roughly.
- **The Suez Canal** from 17 November 1869 and **the Panama Canal** from 15 August 1914, from Natural Earth's lines, which UCHRONIA's rivers leave out.

### The file

`geography.json` is `{ land: { hi, lo }, lakes: { only, add }, marshes, rivers: { drop, add } }`. Times are decimal years, as in the page, and `null` is open-ended:

- `land[level]`: patches `{ g, f, t, k, s, h, x, a, d, b }`, where `g` names the change, `f` and `t` are the first and the end time, `k` is the polygon's key `[length, lon, lat]`, and `b` is the box to redraw in the coast grid.
- `lakes.only`: `[name, km², from, to]` for lakes of `lakes.json` shown only between two times.
- `lakes.add`: `[name, km², from, to, MultiPolygon]` for past extents.
- `rivers.drop`: `[index, length, lon, lat]` for lines of `rivers.json` replaced here.
- `rivers.add`: `[name, rank, [[from, to], …], line]`.

### Building the geography

```
mkdir ne cbs
for f in ne_10m_lakes ne_10m_lakes_europe ne_10m_lakes_north_america ne_10m_lakes_historic ne_10m_rivers_lake_centerlines; do
  curl -L -o ne/$f.geojson https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/master/geojson/$f.geojson
done
curl -L -o ne/ne_10m_lakes_v4.1.0.geojson https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/v4.1.0/geojson/ne_10m_lakes.geojson
curl -L -o cbs/gemeente_1995.geojson https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cartomap/nl/gh-pages/wgs84/gemeente_1995.geojson
curl -L -o cbs/buurt_2011.geojson https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cartomap/nl/gh-pages/wgs84/buurt_2011.geojson
npm i topojson-client d3-geo polygon-clipping @turf/buffer
node chronia/tools/build-geography.mjs node_modules ne cbs
```

It takes a few seconds. An optional fourth argument points at Natural Earth 4.1's lakes, by default `ne_10m_lakes_v4.1.0.geojson` in the Natural Earth folder. With `GEO_LOG=file` it writes how each reservoir was dated.

The polders come from Statistics Netherlands' municipalities of 1995: the Wieringermeer; the Noordoostpolder (without Urk, which stays an island); Eastern Flevoland, which is Dronten and Lelystad without its Oostvaardersplassen neighbourhood; and Southern Flevoland, which is Zeewolde, Almere, and the Oostvaardersplassen, since the Knardijk between the two polders runs along the edge of that neighbourhood and the boundary of Zeewolde. The old land is every other municipality, with strips under 800 m wide removed so that dikes like the Afsluitdijk don't close the sea. The Zuiderzee is the IJsselmeer and the undrained polders, widened by 6 km, less the old land, inside a box from 4.8° to 6.05° E and 52.2° to 53.15° N. The past IJsselmeer is today's lake joined to the undrained polders, widened by 600 m to close the gaps along the dikes.

## The year

- **Time:** the page keeps one number, the time in decimal years. Before 1886 it is a whole year. From 1886 it stands for a day: the 15th for a month, unless a change or a typed date gives another day. A year typed on its own from 1886 means July of that year.
- **The date shown:** the box shows the time as exactly as it is set: a year before 1886 ("1815", "500 BC"); from 1886 a month ("July 1960", the 15th), or a full date for any other day ("30 June 1960", "25 June 1991"). Nothing else is shown under it. `__chronia.asof()` still gives the source of the borders on the map, for tests: "Borders since 1772" until 1885, the latest year on or before the one shown in which a border changed; "Borders since 30 June 1960" from 1886; and "The latest borders, of 2019" after the data ends.
- **The scale:** it is uneven, so that recent times get room. It is set by `KNOTS`: 3400 BC at 0, 500 BC at 0.12, AD 500 at 0.22, 1200 at 0.31, 1400 at 0.37, 1800 at 0.53, 1886 at 0.6, and the end of 2026 at 1, so every year from 1886 has the same room. The slider has 10,000 steps.
- **Ticks:** above the line, a faint mark for each change until 1885 and from 1886, and the mark of the change the borders date from in red.
- **Snapping:** dragging moves in steps of 10 years before AD 1000, single years from then to 1885, and months from 1886. Home goes to 3400 BC.
- **What is shown:**
  - Until 1885, the spans that hold in that year.
  - From 1886, the spans that hold on that day.
  - After 2019, the last day of the data.
- **Loading:**
  - Until 1885, the century's file loads when needed (the files before 1200 are 0.06 to 1.2 MB each, 18.5 MB in all, or 5.4 MB compressed; from 1200, 1.1 to 5.6 MB, or 0.3 to 1.8 MB compressed, 22.1 MB in all; the 1700 and 1800 files are the largest), and the centuries before and after load next. Opening at 1815 therefore downloads the 1800 file (5.6 MB) after `eras.json` and `modern.json` (3.9 MB), then the 1700 file (4.9 MB). A year's set of spans becomes a map, and the last 24 sets are kept, as from 1886.
  - `modern.json` loads at the start. A day's set of spans becomes a map, and the last 24 sets are kept.
  - Shapes are converted once per span, so moving between sets only recomputes the borders. Scrubbing through a century from 1886 takes about 100 ms in headless Chromium.
- **Play:** `PLAY` holds every pace in one place: `yearlyFrom` (1200, where play turns from changes to years), `early` (the wait at each change before 1200), `map`, `year`, `yearChange`, `month`, and `monthChange`, in milliseconds. `playNext(t)` gives the next time to show and `playWait(from, to)` how long to stay on it, holding when one of the change points (`points`: the change years until 1885 and the changes from 1886) falls between the two. Until 1885, play waits for the century's file to load before it steps on, checking every 100 ms (`load`), so no year is passed over while its borders load. Double speed halves every wait in `playDelay`.

The page's `dated` object holds the years (`from`, `to`), the change years (`changes`), and the century files (`chunks`); `isDated(t)`, `chunkIndexFor(y)`, `loadChunk(k)`, `datedSnap(k, y)`, and `datedChangeBefore(y)` do for these years what `modern`, `modernSnap`, and `changeBefore` do from 1886. The change years go into the same list of change points as the snapshot maps and the changes from 1886, which `stepMap`, the arrow keys, and play use, and they get ticks of class `is-change`, kept in `datedTicks`.

## The card

- **The heading:** for a state, whether it is a state, a colony, protectorate, mandate, occupied territory, or other dependency, or a people or culture, and its name. A dependency from Cliopatria or the small territories is called a dependency, or by its status from the table. For a city, whether it is a capital, a city, or a town, and its name at the date.
- **What the current map says:**
  - For a state, one fact a line, as "Label: value": what it is disputed between, who holds it, and what it is part of, each with a link that opens that state's card; its capital, in force on the day from 1886 under its name at the time (Lagos for Nigeria in 1980), and in the year shown from 1400 to 1885 where the data has one; its largest city, the most populous city of the city table inside its land at that time (or just off its coast, when no other state's land holds it), under its name at the time; its flag, as a small picture; its official languages, up to four; and its current borders ("Borders from: June 16, 1910" and "Borders until: June 29, 1960" from 1886, years from 1200 to 1885). The flag and the languages come from Wikidata, from the item of the state's Wikipedia article for that year (so Libya in 1913 is Italian Libya; `CS_WIKI_BY_YEAR` in the builder gives the colonial and mandate titles of Egypt, Libya, Algeria, Tunisia, Morocco, Eritrea, Lebanon, Syria, and Iraq): the flag (P41) and the official languages (P37) whose start and end dates hold at the date shown, or those without dates when none do, with the preferred ones first. A language official only in part of the state (P518 or P3005, such as Hawaiian in Hawaii) is left out, and an official language dated from a later law (French in France, from 1992) counts for the years before. When no language is official for the whole state, as in the United States, the card gives the language used (P2936) that Wikidata ranks preferred, or the one or two with the most speakers. Nothing is taken from an item for a date before its inception (P571) or after its dissolution (P576), unless the article was chosen for those years by a title that differs from the state's name (Italian Libya, whose item begins in 1934, for Libya in 1913). The statements the card reads, and the languages' names, are kept in the browser's `localStorage` for a week (`chronia-wd:` and `chronia-wdl:` keys), so a card opened again asks Wikidata nothing. The flag's picture is a thumbnail from Wikimedia Commons, through Wikipedia's API, served from `thumb.wikimedia.org` (allowed in `img-src`, with `upload.wikimedia.org`). A line whose data is missing is left out. If the state is not on the current map, the card says so. States with precision 1 carry "Its borders here are approximate". With the divisions layer on, it names the first-level division under the click (see [First-level divisions](#first-level-divisions-1)). Changing the date closes the card, since it describes one date.
  - For a city: its name today, if it differs ("Now Gdańsk"), and the state it lies in on the date, or whose capital it is ("Capital of Free City of Danzig on 15 July 1925", or "Capital of Kingdom of Spain in 1700" from 1200 to 1885), with a link that opens that state's card. A capital's name today comes from the city table, matched by place.
- **The note:** a paragraph of 80 to 140 words about the subject as it was at that date and in the wider period, written by DeepSeek (V4.1 Flash), or by Claude (Haiku 5.5) when DeepSeek can't answer, through a server function, `supabase/functions/chronia-blurb` (see [The note's server function](#the-notes-server-function)). It is written only when someone opens the card, then kept, so everyone who opens the same card after reads the same note and nothing more is spent. While it is written the card shows nothing in its place, and the card does not say which model wrote it.
  - The page sends only what the card shows: for a state, its name, its Wikipedia title for the year, its status, ruler, the state it is part of, its capital, the first day of its current borders (from 1886), the names of up to ten states and peoples it borders on the map, largest first (`neighbourNames`), and the year; for a city, its name at the date, its name today, its place, the state it lies in, whether it is that state's capital, and the year. Before 1886 the year is the one in the year box, not the map's.
  - When the card is opened by a click (on the map, on a state named in a card, or through `__chronia.selectName`), the note is written if it does not exist. When the card is already open and the date changes, nothing is written: the card shows the Wikipedia summary and offers "Write about Prussia in 1878" as a link, and after the date has stayed put for 0.7 seconds it asks the function, with `peek: true`, whether a note for the new date already exists, and shows it if so. Playing through time with a card open therefore writes nothing.
  - The page keeps the notes it has fetched, by request, for as long as it is open, so reopening a card asks nothing.
  - If the function fails, is over its daily cap, can't be reached (as until it is deployed), or takes more than 28 seconds, the card falls back to the Wikipedia summary, with no note under it.
- **Wikipedia:** the card fetches the summary of the article named by the builder's title for that map's year, from `https://en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/summary/`, for the link, the thumbnail, and the fallback. For a city it looks up its name at the date, where that differs from its name today (Londinium, Constantinople), then its name today, and takes an article only if the article's coordinates lie within 60 km of the city (`WIKI_NEAR_KM`); failing both, it takes the first of Wikipedia's top five search results for its name today that lies that close, or else shows that Wikipedia has no article on the place. So the Roman town of Iesso finds Guissona, the town on its site, rather than Ezo, the old name of Hokkaido that "Iesso" redirects to.
  - For a state, if there is none, or it is a disambiguation page, the card tries the plain name, then Wikipedia's search.
  - The card shows the article's thumbnail, sepia-toned, and links to the article below the note. When the article's title differs from the name on the card, the link names it.
  - Only `upload.wikimedia.org` images and `en.wikipedia.org` links are used.

All text from the function and from Wikipedia goes into the page as text, never as HTML.

These requests go from the visitor's browser to Wikipedia and to the site's Supabase project when a state or a city is clicked. The function counts new notes per visitor under a salted hash of the visitor's address and the day, and deletes the counts each day. Section 8 of the site's [privacy page](../privacy/) describes both, and section 3 the requests to Wikipedia.

### The note's server function

`supabase/functions/chronia-blurb/index.ts` is a Supabase edge function (Deno), built like UCHRONIA's Historian: it answers only the site's origins (the same list as Historian's: `https://noahdarwinlee.com`, `https://www.noahdarwinlee.com`, `https://noahdlee519.github.io`, and `http://localhost` or `http://127.0.0.1` on any port), checks every field, and calls DeepSeek's chat completions API with the `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY` secret, or, if that is unset or the call fails, Anthropic's Messages API with the `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` secret. It is one file with no imports. Its table and counters are set up by `supabase/migrations/20261026_chronia_blurbs.sql`.

- **The request:** a POST of JSON, `{ kind: "state", name, wiki, status, ruler, partOf, capital, since, neighbours, year }`, `{ kind: "city", name, modern, lon, lat, state, capital, year }`, or `{ kind: "division", name, country, year }` for a country's state, province, or territory, with `peek: true` to ask only for a note already written. Names are at most 80 characters (a Wikipedia title 120), must contain a letter, and may hold only letters, marks, digits, spaces, and the punctuation found in the atlas's names (`. , ' ’ ‘ ʼ ( ) - – & / : · • ‧ ꞉ [ ] " ? ^`), so no markup, braces, backticks, hashes, or line breaks reach the model. `status` is one of `state`, `colony`, `protectorate`, `mandate`, `occupied`, `dependency`, or `people`; `year` is a whole year from 3000 BC (`-3000`) to 2026, not 0; `since` is a date from 1886; `neighbours` is a list of at most ten names, and a name that fails the check is dropped rather than refusing the request; `lon` and `lat` are numbers in range and are rounded to 0.01°. A body over 4,000 characters is refused. Anything else is a 400 naming the field.
- **The period:** each note is written for a period, not a day, so that it can be shared: from 1886 a state's note is for its year; a city's from 1886 is for its decade; before 1886, both are for a decade from 1500, a quarter-century from 1000, a half-century from AD 1, and a century BC ("the 1810s", "from 1275 to 1299", "from AD 800 to 849", "from 400 to 301 BC"). Before 1886 the borders change by the year at most and the record thins out, so a note per year would cost thousands of near-identical notes per state; from 1886 the borders are dated to the day and a year matters (1914, 1918, and 1919 are different worlds), but a city changes more slowly than the states around it. The model is given the period, never the exact year, so the note fits every date it is shown for.
- **The cache key:** the kind, the name, the period's first year, and the first 16 hex digits of a SHA-256 of the prompt's version (`PROMPT_VERSION`, 2 since the change to DeepSeek, so every subject is written afresh once) and every field the model sees but the neighbours, after checking and rounding, for example `s:prussia:1810:f1119762b36c9fca` (`c:` for a city, `d:` for a division). CHRONIA's own ids are not used, because they are indexes into `eras.json` and move when the data is rebuilt. The neighbours are left out of the key because they change from year to year within a period, and the note is for the period. Because the key covers every other fact in the prompt, a request with altered facts gets a note of its own and can never replace the one honest requests share; and a state whose status, ruler, or capital changes within a period gets a note for each.
- **The models:** first DeepSeek's `deepseek-flash` (V4.1 Flash; `DEEPSEEK_MODEL` overrides it), with thinking off (`thinking: { type: "disabled" }`), temperature 0.6, at most 400 tokens, and 16 seconds to answer. Without `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY` but with `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, the same model is reached through OpenRouter as `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash` (`OPENROUTER_MODEL` overrides it), with reasoning off (`reasoning: { enabled: false }`) and the same limits. Then, if neither key is set, its call fails, or its text is unusable, Claude's `claude-haiku-5-5` (`CHRONIA_CLAUDE_MODEL` overrides it), with thinking off (`thinking: { type: "disabled" }`; the first deployment's `between_tools` and `output_config` made every call fail with a 400), the system prompt cached, and 10 seconds. Both answers fit within the page's 28-second wait. The prompt asks for one paragraph of 80 to 140 words of plain prose in British spelling with the serial comma: for a state, what it was then, who ruled it, what was happening around then, and where it stood; for a people, what is known of them and what the evidence is; for a city, what it was, whose, what it was known for, and what was happening there. It asks the note to tie the subject to its era: to name the larger story it belonged to, as historians name it (the Mongol conquests, the Reformation, the Scramble for Africa), to mention one or two of the neighbours given that mattered to it then, and to prefer the concrete detail of the period to description that could fit any century. It must say plainly when the record is thin, never present a guess as fact or later events as known at the time, never repeat a fact from the atlas it knows to be wrong, and write about the whole period. The subject's details go in the user turn as JSON, and the prompt says they are data, never instructions; a subject it can't identify gets the single word `UNKNOWN`, which is not kept.
- **The text:** the function strips headings, list marks, emphasis, tags, control characters, and quotation marks round the whole note, folds it into one paragraph, cuts it at a sentence end if it runs over 170 words or stops mid-sentence, and refuses one under 25 words. It doesn't stream: a note takes a few seconds, and checking the whole text before it is kept or shown is worth more than showing it word by word.
- **The answers:** 200 with `{ text, model, period, cached }` (a peek that finds nothing returns `text: null`); 400 for a bad field; 413 for a long body; 429 with `error: "cap"` when the site has written its day's notes, or `"limit"` when the visitor has; 502 when both models fail or their text is unusable, with `status` (the model API's HTTP status) and `detail` (the first 240 characters of its error, with anything like a key masked), so a failing deployment can be diagnosed from the page; and 502 with `error: "empty"`, the model, and `why` (its stop reason, its content blocks, and how many words it wrote, with the first 60 characters) when its text is refused; 503 when neither key is set (notes already written are still served); 500 when the database can't be reached; 403, with no CORS headers, for any other origin or none.
- **The caps and the cost:** at most 500 new notes a day for the whole site (`ALL_CAP`), and 30 a day for one visitor (`WHO_CAP`). Notes already written are free and are never counted. A note's prompt is about 750 tokens in (a 680-token system prompt and the subject) and 110 to 190 tokens out, at most 400. At DeepSeek's list price for V4.1 Flash in October 2026, $0.30 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens at peak hours and half that off-peak, a note costs about $0.0005 at most. Claude Haiku 5.5, at $0.10 and $0.50 per million on Anthropic's pricing page in October 2026 (its tokenizer counts about 30% more tokens), costs about $0.0002, so the backup is in fact the cheaper of the two; Sonnet 5.5, which wrote the notes before, cost about $0.003. 500 notes a day is therefore at most about $0.25 a day. 30 a visitor is enough for anyone reading around the map, and keeps one visitor to 6% of the day's notes. A failed call to the model gives its count back; a refusal or an unusable answer, which costs tokens, does not.
- **The visitor:** the visitor is the first address in `x-forwarded-for` (or `x-real-ip`), IPv6 by its /64, hashed with SHA-256 together with a salt (`CHRONIA_SALT`, or the service key when that is unset) and the day, and cut to 24 hex digits. The first new note of each day (UTC) deletes the earlier days' counts, so no count outlives its day. A request with no usable address counts only towards the site's cap.
- **Two at once:** two requests in the same function instance for the same note make one call to the model.
- **The tables:** `chronia_blurbs` (`key`, `kind`, `subject`, `period`, `text`, `model`, `created_at`) and `chronia_blurb_usage` (`day`, `who`, `n`), both with row-level security on and no access for `anon` or `authenticated`; `chronia_blurb_take`, `chronia_blurb_give_back`, and `chronia_blurb_save`, callable only by `service_role`. `chronia_blurb_save` keeps the first note written under a key and returns it. To withdraw a bad note, delete its row and the next click writes it afresh; `select key, period, left(text, 60) from chronia_blurbs order by created_at desc limit 20;` lists the latest.

### Deploying the notes

The notes are not live until the function is deployed to the Supabase project `kigvciyyjlgjcgnwgrwf`, the one Historian uses. Until then the card falls back to the Wikipedia summary.

1. **The migration:** apply `supabase/migrations/20261026_chronia_blurbs.sql` (named `20261026_chronia_blurbs`), then `supabase/migrations/20261028_chronia_blurbs_divisions.sql`, which lets the table hold notes on divisions, with `supabase db push`, the SQL editor, or the Supabase MCP's `apply_migration`. It only creates new objects and touches nothing of Historian's, and it is safe to run twice.
2. **The function:** deploy `supabase/functions/chronia-blurb` with JWT verification off, since the page sends no Authorization header and no Supabase key, and the function checks the origin, the fields, and its own caps:

   ```
   supabase functions deploy chronia-blurb --no-verify-jwt --project-ref kigvciyyjlgjcgnwgrwf
   ```

   or the MCP's `deploy_edge_function` with `name: "chronia-blurb"`, `verify_jwt: false`, and the file as `index.ts`. The page calls `https://kigvciyyjlgjcgnwgrwf.supabase.co/functions/v1/chronia-blurb` (`BLURB_URL` in `app.js`); if the function ends up under another slug, change `BLURB_URL` to match. The CSP needs no change, since the host is already in `connect-src`.
3. **The secrets:** `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is already set for Historian, and function secrets are project-wide, so Claude can write the notes at once. For DeepSeek, create a key at platform.deepseek.com and add it as `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY` under Edge Functions, Secrets (or `supabase secrets set DEEPSEEK_API_KEY=…`); until then every note is written by Claude. `CHRONIA_SALT` is optional but recommended, any long random string (`supabase secrets set CHRONIA_SALT=$(openssl rand -hex 32)`); without it the function salts the visitor counter with the service key, which also works. `SUPABASE_URL` and the service key are given to every function by Supabase.
4. **A check:** a peek costs nothing and writes nothing:

   ```
   curl -s -X POST https://kigvciyyjlgjcgnwgrwf.supabase.co/functions/v1/chronia-blurb \
     -H 'origin: https://noahdarwinlee.com' -H 'content-type: application/json' \
     -d '{"kind":"state","name":"Prussia","wiki":"Kingdom of Prussia","status":"state","capital":"Berlin","year":1815,"peek":true}'
   ```

   It should answer `{"text":null,"period":"the 1810s","cached":false}`. The same without `"peek":true` writes the first note (well under a tenth of a cent) and answers `cached: false`; again, `cached: true`. The usage line in the function's logs gives the tokens. Then open CHRONIA, click a state and a city, and check the note and the line under it, which names the model that wrote it.

## Known limits

- **Neighbours in one colour:** 25 pairs of touching lineages share a colour at some time, because both take a fixed colour from the map of the present (see Colours). Most meet through colonies, since a dependency takes its ruler's colour: French Algeria and Morocco until 1912, British colonies and the Ottoman Empire, Britain's West African colonies and Senegal, and France and Sweden around 1811. The build lists them all.
- **Snapshots before 1200:** where Cliopatria has no state, the land comes from the nearest snapshot map, so it changes only halfway between two maps, which are up to a thousand years apart before 1000 BC: the map of 3000 BC fills 3400 to 2501 BC. Names on those maps can be out of their time at the ends of their stretch, and many are fixed (see [Names fixed](#names-fixed)), but not all.
- **Before 1200:** Cliopatria's states before 1200 have firm borders, as its later ones do, though many ancient borders were zones; the cultures and peoples of the snapshots fill the rest. Its dates are often 25 or 50 years long before AD 500.
- **Cliopatria's dates:** its spans are years, often five or ten years long before 1800, so a change can show a few years late or early, and the changes it adds after 1886 are dated to 1 January.
- **Cliopatria's coverage:** before about 1850 it leaves much of the world blank, and the fill comes from the nearest historical-basemaps map, which can be decades away. Its colonial empires are often one shape for all their colonies.
- **Cliopatria's steps:** its borders follow a grid in steps 7 to 20 km wide. They take CShapes' lines of 1886 back to the last change of border (see [Meeting the lines of 1886](#meeting-the-lines-of-1886)), but before that, and wherever a border was then still to move by more than a sliver, they keep the steps, so a border's detail changes in the year it moved. The coasts keep Cliopatria's line.
- **Ireland before 1801:** Cliopatria draws Ireland in the Kingdom of Great Britain, where historical-basemaps had a Kingdom of Ireland.
- **Member states:** a state is drawn out of an umbrella or a larger state only where a historical-basemaps map names it, so its own border changes only at those maps, and one that no map names stays inside the larger shape. Some shapes are left as they are: Cliopatria's Kingdom of England takes in the Scottish Lowlands from about 1660 to 1706, so Edinburgh is in England in 1700; Hanover is in the Holy Roman Empire itself until the 1700s; Dresden is in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth in 1700 and 1750 (Saxony's elector was then also Poland's king); and the spot where Liechtenstein is drawn from 1719 is in the Swiss Confederation before it. The regression check under [Testing](#testing) still flags 81 states, most of them finer splits.
- **CShapes' coverage:** it records only states and recognised dependencies. The builder fills the land it leaves out from Cliopatria, from later CShapes shapes, from the table of small territories, and from historical-basemaps, but none of them claims the central Sahara, much of the Kalahari and the upper Zambezi, or the land between the Ubangi and the upper Nile around 1890, so these stay blank until the colonial borders reach them, as does Antarctica. The backfilled shapes are the later borders, so the Emirate of Nejd of 1886 has Saudi Arabia's border of 1932 where no other source draws it.
- **Blank land:** the share of land outside Antarctica in no state and no people, before the yearly data and the fill from Cliopatria and after: 1400, 22% and 21%; 1453, 22% and 14%; 1600, 29% and 27%; 1700, 29% and 27%; 1772, 28% and 18%; 1815, 21% and 18%; 1848, 21% and 14%; 1885, 15% and 11%; 1890, 16% and 11%; 1911, 3.7% and 1.8%; 1930, 3.8% and 0.9%; 1950 and 2000, 2.1% and 0.4%. From 1886 the later shapes, the small territories, and historical-basemaps then fill much of what is left: of the 15,312 cells of land in a 1° grid outside Antarctica, the cells in no state fall from 1,209 to 778 in 1890, from 171 to 59 in 1900, from 133 to 48 in 1925 (Svalbard, Franz Josef Land, and Severnaya Zemlya before they were claimed), and from 25 to none in 1950 and 2010. Before 1886, keeping the large uncovered pieces fills 36 cells in 1800 and 56 in 1850.
- **CShapes and the Second World War:** the Gleditsch and Ward edition dates the changes that were settled by treaty or annexation, such as Munich (30 September 1938), the First Vienna Award (2 November 1938), Vilnius (1 October 1939), the Winter War (12 March 1940), the Baltic states (2 June 1940), Bessarabia (28 June 1940), Southern Dobruja (7 September 1940), and the settlement of 8 May 1945. It does not have the end of Czechoslovakia in March 1939, Memel (March 1939), Albania (April 1939), the partition of Poland (September 1939), the occupations of 1940 and 1941, or the Saar under the League (1920 to 1935). The Correlates of War edition has some of these, but only as the whole state "occupied" by one power, which is wrong for Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, Greece, and Albania, so they are not used.
- **Treaty dates:** CShapes dates the First World War settlements by the day each treaty was signed, such as Versailles on 28 June 1919, not the day it came into force.
- **CShapes' capitals:** each span has one capital, so a capital that moved within a span is shown in its later place. `CAP_BEFORE` corrects the cases listed under [From 1886](#from-1886); any others still show the later capital.
- **Capitals before 1886:** they are only as good as Wikidata's statements. Many states have none, or several with no dates, and get no capital; some statements are a later capital with no dates, which is then shown for every year the dated ones leave open. Before 1400 the data has no capitals, so a capital shows only as a city.
- **After 2019:** CShapes ends on 31 December 2019, and the map stays as it was then. No internationally recognised border has changed since, but claimed annexations are not shown.
- **Play** visits the 15th of each month from 1886, so a span shorter than a month that holds no 15th is passed over. There are nine, such as the Soviet Union's last few days in December 1991. Until 1885 it holds on the change years, which are only as exact as Cliopatria's years.
- **Coasts:** where a historical shape stops well short of the coast, more than about 20 km, the land between is left as paper. Small islands that a source leaves out show as paper.
- **Small territories:** Vatican City is in the data from 1929, but Natural Earth's shape of it is a few hundred metres across and collapses at the map's finest level of detail (about 200 m), so it can't be seen or clicked. Portuguese India is Goa only; Daman, Diu, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli share one Natural Earth province with each other and are left in India.
- **State names:** a name is placed only where it fits inside its shape, slightly over its border for a large state, or offshore for a small one, so the names of small states inland are dropped when they don't fit. Names along steep parallels near the edge of the globe are hidden until the globe is turned. A large state's name is placed at fixed spots on the ground, so when the map is zoomed far into a state between its spots, its name can be off the screen.
- **The sources' own errors:** there is more to correct in the builder's tables. Among the errors found in Cliopatria and left in place, its "British Africa" of 1885 (now named the Khedivate of Egypt, under the United Kingdom) still holds the Sudan, the year the Mahdists took Khartoum, and its Kingdom of Portugal includes Angola and Mozambique in the 1880s.
- **Names left as they are:** historical-basemaps' map of 1492 draws Native Land's present-day nations, reservations included ("Cherokee (Oklahoma)", "Hoopa (Reservation)"), as the nations of 1446 to 1499 (drawn as peoples since 27 October 2026); a few of its states of 1400 are out of date (Vladimir-Suzdal), with no state of the time to give their land to (Kediri and the Chola Empire now end in 1240 and 1279); Cliopatria's "House of Ascania" is left, since its land and dates fit neither Saxe-Wittenberg nor Anhalt; and "Malaya" stays as a region named as a state ("Arabia" from 1847 is left out, and the rest of Madagascar beside the Merina Kingdom is the Malagasy, a people, since 27 October 2026). Cliopatria's colonies in the Americas are one Spanish Empire, where an atlas would name the viceroyalties. A shape from a map is used up to halfway to the map before, so the 1880 map's states start in 1847 (the Toucouleur Empire before its founding in 1852).
- **Cities through time:** the populations are estimates, often a single figure for a century or more, and Chandler's lists name only the largest cities of each date, so smaller cities come and go with the data. Many of Hanson's estimates come from the areas inside city walls and can be high (Phigalia, Maronea). Some cities are still geocoded wrongly; `REBA_FIX` holds the corrections found so far.
- **Founding dates:** a city with no founding date appears 100 years before its first figure, so some appear too early or too late.
- **Cities with no history in the sources:** about 840 cities of today with no figure before 1950 and no Wikidata history appear in 1850, the earliest the rule allows for them, and about 550 whose first figure falls between 1850 and 1900 appear in 1800 for the same reason, so zoomed in close, those years still bring a crowd. Outside the United States, a city's early figures are the sources' estimates, often for the agglomeration rather than the city.
- **City names:** about 550 cities have names by date (420 from the table, the rest by the rule for Hanson's cities); most others keep today's name throughout, or their ancient name. The switch from ancient to modern names by region is approximate.
- **Americas before 1500:** the sources hold few cities there (Teotihuacan, Tikal, Tollan, Cahokia, Tenochtitlan, Texcoco, Cusco, and a few more).
- **First-level divisions:** they are shown only for the United States from 1783, and for Canada, Australia, Mexico, Germany, Austria, China, and India from the dates their present boundaries have held. Germany's eastern states are drawn as they are now from 1990, although a few small areas, such as Amt Neuhaus (moved from Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania to Lower Saxony in 1993), changed state in 1992 and 1993. A division's lines stop at the border of the country on the current map, so where the borders lag behind the divisions, as they can where Cliopatria's dates are years long or a fill comes from a map decades away, part of a division's lines may not be drawn.
- **Geography over time:** only the changes listed under [Geography over time](#geography-over-time) are made. The coastline elsewhere is today's at every date: the growth of the Yellow River's and other deltas, the reclamations of Tokyo Bay, Singapore, Hong Kong, and Dubai, and the draining of the smaller Dutch lakes are not shown, either because no dated outline was at hand or because Natural Earth's land, simplified to about 1.6 km and with Honshu in fewer than 300 points, is too coarse to show them. Lakes and the Zuiderzee change on one day, though most filled or shrank over years. Between its mapped extents, the Aral Sea is drawn as it was in 2003 and 2004 from 1987 to 2014, so 1990 shows it too small and 2010 too large. Lop Nur's historic extent is smaller than the 2,400 km² of 1930. Wieringen is drawn as an island until 1930, though a dike joined it to the mainland in 1924. The Yellow River's course from 1128 to 1855 is a straight-lined approximation. The flooding of 1938 to 1947, when the Yellow River ran south to the Huai after the dikes at Huayuankou were breached, is not shown.
- **The notes:** they are written by a model, so they can be wrong, especially for peoples and early periods where the record is thin, and a wrong one stays until its row in `chronia_blurbs` is deleted. Each is written once for a period, not a day, so a note on Danzig in the 1920s is shown for every date in that decade.
- **Cities' cards:** a city's state is the state under its mark, found from the point or, for a city on the coast, from up to 10 px around it; a city just off the land at Natural Earth's resolution may be shown "in no state".
- **Wikipedia:** for peoples and obscure names, the search fallback can find a loosely related article. The link always names the article it found.
