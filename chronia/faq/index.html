Using the map

Why do the borders change by the year in some periods and by the day in others?

The sources get finer as they get nearer today. Before 1648 the borders change by the year; from 1648, the Peace of Westphalia, by the month; and from 1800 by the day, so a treaty or an annexation takes effect on the day it did. The date in its frame shows a year, a month, or a day to match.

How do I jump to a date or find a place?

Type a date in its frame at the top (a year such as 1648 or 500 BC, or a month such as June 1914) and press Enter, or drag the marker along the timeline at the bottom. The arrow keys step back and forward, and P plays the years by. To find a state, a people, or a city, open Search (the magnifying glass at the top right) or press /.

How do I get to UCHRONIA?

On the atlas, open Save (the floppy disk at the top right; on a phone, under Menu) and choose Open in UCHRONIA. That takes the map you are looking at, at its date, into UCHRONIA, the map editor, to redraw it as you like. Or go straight to UCHRONIA.

How do I show zones of control in wars?

On the atlas, open View (the stacked layers at the top right; on a phone, under Menu) and tick Zones of control under “On the map”. The map then colors the ground each side held, month by month, in the wars the layer covers, with the front between them in dark red; in years without one of those wars it shows nothing more. Untick it to go back to the states’ borders.

What do the italic names, dotted lines, and stripes mean?

A name in italics with no colour is a people or culture rather than a state; a faint italic name is a band with no fixed border. A dotted border is unsettled or approximate, and land striped in two states’ colours is disputed between them. The Key at the bottom left lists every sign on the map you are looking at.

Can I see a flat map instead of the globe?

Yes. Open View and choose Flat map, or press M to switch between the two. On the globe, [ and ] turn it and N sets north back up.

Is CHRONIA in other languages?

Yes, in 14: English, Spanish, French, German, Dutch, Italian, Portuguese, Czech, Polish, Turkish, Russian, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean. It follows your browser’s language, and you can change it under Language at the bottom of View.

Where it comes from

Where do the borders come from?

From published research datasets, not drawn by hand: Cliopatria (the Seshat Global History Databank) to 1885, historical-basemaps for the land it leaves blank, mostly peoples, and CShapes 2.0 from 1886, with dated tables written for CHRONIA where those are coarse or wrong. Sources and method says which source draws which years and how they fit together.

How accurate is it?

As accurate as its sources and the corrections made to them, and no more: it is a work of research and judgement, not a legal or official record of any border. Every change made to the sources is recorded in a ledger with its own sources, how confident it is, and the other readings that were possible. Older maps are rougher than recent ones, and the farther back you go, the more the lines are estimates.

I found a mistake. How do I report it?

Open the flag at the top right (on a phone, under Menu), sign in with Google, choose the place on the map, set the dates it is wrong for, and say what is wrong and how you know. The team reads every report; reports from accounts with Premium are read first.

Who writes the notes on the cards?

An AI model writes a short note on a state, a people, or a city the first time someone opens its card, from what the atlas shows about it and nothing about you. The note is then kept, and everyone who opens that card later reads the same one. Each reader can have 40 new notes written a day (200 with Premium); notes already written are free.

Your account and Premium

How do I make a timelapse video?

Sign in with Google, open Save, and under Timelapse video type a first and a last date (up to 500 years apart), choose a frame for each month or each year and a speed, and press Make the video. The atlas draws each date in turn and downloads an MP4. It is made in your own browser, so it is never sent anywhere. Videos made without Premium carry a small CHRONIA mark in the corner.

What does CHRONIA Premium get me?

Premium is a single payment of $10, made with the Google account you sign in with, and it stays with that account for good. It takes the advertisements off the atlas and the watermark off your timelapse videos and saved images (and lets you leave the date off an image), gives you five times the daily notes on the cards (200 in place of 40) and 25 questions a day to UCHRONIA’s historian (in place of 5), and puts your reports of errors first in the queue. See CHRONIA Premium for the details and to buy it.

Why do I need to sign in?

Only to report a mistake, to make a timelapse video, and for Premium; everything else works without an account. Signing in with Google keeps your name and email address, and CHRONIA never posts anything for you. The privacy policy says what is kept and for how long.

Can I use CHRONIA’s maps in my own work?

Yes: you may use the atlas in teaching, study, writing, and work, take screenshots, and quote it, with credit to CHRONIA, and you need no permission for that. What you may not do is copy its data or code in bulk or use them to build a copy of the atlas, except as a source’s own licence allows. See the terms, section 3.
