# Country tags: merged proposal for Noah

## The rule used
- Two names are one country when historians treat them as the same state carrying on: the same state under another name, regime, or dynasty (Kingdom of France, French First Republic, and France).
- A state and the country it directly became, including a colony or protectorate and the independent state on essentially the same land (Gold Coast and Ghana).
- A union named for its senior partner goes with that partner (Denmark-Norway under Denmark).
- Not together: a conqueror and the different state it replaced, two names drawn as separate states at the same time for more than five years, a people with a state, or names that only share a place name.
- A new tag needs two names on the map, and I counted any name present in the data, dated or not. Where the existing 56 tags give a precedent, it wins.

## Your call
1. **Fold the separate Zhou, Northern Wei and Western Wei tags into China?** For: the brief counts the dynasties that ruled China as one country, and the tag already holds Yuan and Manchu rulers. Against: the China tag starts at Qin, and the Wei lines ran beside the Southern dynasties, which I did add to China.
   Recommend: fold Zhou and Shang into China, keep the two Wei tags separate (low).
2. **Three Kingdoms, Ten Kingdoms, Southern Ming and similar rivals** For: each is conventionally a Chinese dynasty, and Cao Wei leads straight to Western Jin, which is tagged. Against: the brief says rivals drawn at once do not share a tag, and Xia is dated 2070 BC to 438, which is wrong.
   Recommend: add Cao Wei alone, leave the rest out.
3. **Does Ayyubid Sultanate belong to Egypt or to its own Ayyubids tag?** For Egypt: it ruled from Cairo and sits between the Fatimids and the Mamluks. Against: its lands were mostly in Syria and the Jazira, and the cadet branches are not Egypt at all.
   Recommend: keep it as Ayyubids (low) and add the medieval Cairo dynasties to Egypt only at low.
4. **Ptolemaic, Mamluk, Fatimid, and the Second Intermediate Period dynasties** For: Egypt is the continuous country under all of them, and the tag already holds the Twenty-first and Twenty-second dynasties. Against: Macedonian and Turkic conquerors are not native successors, and several pharaonic dynasties ran at the same time.
   Recommend: add Ptolemaic (medium), the Seventeenth Dynasty (high), and the Cairo dynasties (low); leave the rival pharaonic dynasties out.
5. **Achaemenid, Parthian, and Sasanian empires into Iran?** For: Iran is normally told as one long country, as China is. Against: they are different empires, the Parthians and Sasanians are different dynasties, and the Ilkhanate was Mongol.
   Recommend: add the three empires at medium; leave the Medes, Ilkhanate, and Timurids at low.
6. **British India, the Delhi Sultanate, and the Marathas into India?** For: Gold Coast went with Ghana, and the tag already counts the Mughals. Against: India across 800 years of sultanates, empires, and a colony stretches "same country" a long way.
   Recommend: merge British India into India; leave the Delhi Sultanate separate until you decide on a wider India.
7. **The colony-to-nation tags that follow Gold Coast and Ghana** For: the existing Ghana, Zimbabwe, and Mexico tags already do this, and the names are one place under two flags. Against: some colonies were stitched together (Nigeria, South Africa) or covered only part of the later country.
   Recommend: apply the high and medium ones as a batch; look at the low ones one by one.
8. **Cape Colony into South Africa, while Transvaal, Natal, and the Free State stay apart** For: the Union grew from all four, and Cape Colony alone is a partial parent. Against: the Boer republics were independent states, and the map already keeps them as tags.
   Recommend: leave them separate, and keep South Africa at low.
9. **Czechoslovakia, the Two Sicilies, Norway-Denmark, and Jülich-Berg** For: the brief puts a union under its senior partner, as Denmark-Norway sits under Denmark. Against: the senior partner is unclear for each, and Czechoslovakia belongs to two successor tags.
   Recommend: apply Norway-Denmark and Jülich-Berg; keep Czechoslovakia in Czechia (Slovakia has its own tag), and keep the Two Sicilies in Sicily.
10. **The Holy Roman Empire against the Germany tag** For: both are called Germany in the standard narrative. Against: merging puts the Empire and Prussia under one colour for 500 years, when both are drawn at once.
   Recommend: keep them separate (medium).
11. **Partition duchies and cadet branches of the German lands** For: each is the same dynasty and land, and the tags are lopsided without them. Against: they are separate states drawn at once, so the brief says to leave them out.
   Recommend: leave them out; Powys and Munster lost their tag entirely.
12. **Prussia (1233 to 1282) sits in Germany but is the land of the Old Prussians** For removal: it is the Baltic Prussia under the Teutonic conquest, not the Hohenzollern state (two regions flagged it). Against: the Order's state became the Duchy of Prussia, which is in the tag, so the land is continuous.
   Recommend: remove Prussia from Germany; add the Teutonic Order at low.
13. **The Golden Horde, Great Horde, Blue and White Hordes, Kara-Khanids, Göktürks, and Oirat** For: each pair is one dynasty or ruling house under changing titles. Against: successor hordes ruled at the same time, and the Kazakh Khanate and Kazakhstan are 144 years apart.
   Recommend: accept the high and medium splits; leave Golden Horde, Kazakhstan, and Oirat at low; keep Blue and White Horde out.
14. **Silla, Baekje, and Goguryeo, and the two Koreas** For: Korean history is one narrative, and Goryeo and Taebong go in at medium. Against: the Three Kingdoms were rival states, and both Koreas claim to continue Korea, so adding one is not neutral.
   Recommend: keep the Three Kingdoms as separate tags; add both Koreas or neither, and I lean to neither.
15. **East Germany and the occupation zones with Germany** For: it matches how West Germany is already tagged. Against: the GDR was absorbed in 1990 and the zones are drawn at once.
   Recommend: add East Germany at low; leave the zones out.
16. **How far back Vietnam, Cambodia, and Laos reach** For: the dynasties from Ngô to Nguyễn are textbook, and the tag shows one country across 2,000 years. Against: Âu Lạc and the Trưng sisters predate independence, and the regional lords ruled beside each other.
   Recommend: keep the tag at medium as proposed and add none of the regional lords.
17. **Georgia, Kakheti, and Armenia** For: the bare names are what the map uses for the whole sequence. Against: they overlap everything, so a map reader sees one country drawn twice at once.
   Recommend: keep both, with the overlap noted; keep Kakheti at low.
18. **Wallachia, Moldavia, Transylvania, and the Hungary gap** For: both principalities became Romania, and the Romania tag already holds their union. Against: they were separate states at once and had their own histories, and Hungary's gap cannot be filled from the map.
   Recommend: keep Wallachia and Moldavia as their own tags; leave Hungary as proposed.
19. **Kievan Rus' and its heirs** For: Kiev kept the name, and the proposed tag follows it to 1362. Against: Russia and Ukraine each claim the line, and Chernigov, Smolensk, and Polotsk have equal claim.
   Recommend: keep Kievan Rus' as its own tag (medium) and do not link it to a modern country.
20. **Aragon, León, Navarre, Scotland, Wales, Ireland, and Sicily against Spain, Britain, and Italy** For: the brief gives Castile and Spain one tag, and England and Great Britain one. Against: each was an independent country for centuries, so one tag would hide them.
   Recommend: keep them separate; the tags can be linked later.
21. **Pre-Columbian cultures and peoples as tags** For: they follow one polity or one people across several map entries. Against: the brief says peoples rarely need tags, and the Maya never formed one state.
   Recommend: keep the state-like ones (Inca, Aztec, Chimú, Haudenosaunee) and drop Maya and Tungus.
22. **Canada and Australia as federations of colonies** For: each became part of the later country. Against: they were separate states drawn at once, so I left Upper Canada out of Canada.
   Recommend: leave out, and revisit if you want federation tags.
23. **Caliphate, Oman, Yemen, the Saudi lines, and Aksum** For: each is one long Arab or Ethiopian state under changing titles. Against: the caliphate changed dynasties and was claimed by rivals, and Aksum is far from the Ethiopian Empire.
   Recommend: drop the Caliphate tag, keep Aksum separate, and leave the Omani dynasties alone.
24. **Western Roman Empire, Nicaea, and the Roman provinces** For: the Roman state was one empire under two emperors, and Nicaea claimed to be Rome. Against: the brief says to leave out names drawn at once, and provinces are not countries.
   Recommend: add Western Roman Empire at medium; leave Nicaea and the provinces out.
